\section{The RhizAeon Framework: Principles of Operation}
\label{sec:principles_of_operation}


\RhizAeon detects reticulate evolution by tracking continuous lineage trajectories through sequence space.
Scanning along an alignment from 5\textquotesingle\ to 3\textquotesingle, a non-recombinant genome maintains a stable genetic identity: its closest relatives remain its closest relatives, tracing parallel paths through sequence space.
Recombination breaks this stability.
A mosaic genome travels in the company of one parental lineage, then abruptly shifts allegiance to an alternative donor across the exchanged segment.
To capture these shifts in linear time without constructing trees or sliding windows, \RhizAeon measures the instantaneous affinity of each query sequence against a representative panel of reference lineages at every polymorphic site.

To evaluate affinity at each polymorphic site, \RhizAeon adapts cross-attention, the foundational mechanism of transformer neural networks.
The query sequence acts as the query, while the reference lineages act as keys.
At every position along the genome, the query sequence evaluates which reference lineage it resembles, asking essentially: which lineage should I attend to right now?
Rather than treating all nucleotide matches equally, attention scores are weighted by continuous-time Markov substitution probabilities: sharing a rare derived allele commands strong attention, while common transitions command less.
To prevent private mutations from creating spurious matches, the reference panel includes an ancestral root token that absorbs alleles absent from every contemporary reference.
A softmax function then normalizes these scores into an attention distribution that sums to one across all reference lineages at each position.
Because individual sites fluctuate stochastically, \RhizAeon does not evaluate positions in isolation or smooth them with sliding windows.
Instead, it subtracts each lineage's genome-wide background attention and tracks the cumulative deviation along the chromosome.
In non-recombinant regions, positive and negative fluctuations cancel out, wandering near zero like a random walk.
When a sequence enters a recombinant tract, sustained attention to the donor drives the cumulative curve steadily upward.
At the crossover junction, donor attention ceases and home attention resumes, causing the cumulative curve to peak and descend.
A single crossover forms a sharp triangular arch, whereas an inserted cassette produces a trapezoidal plateau.
The switch points are located directly at the peaks and inflection points of these cumulative attention curves, localizing breakpoints to exact nucleotide coordinates.

The transition from site-by-site attention to continuous trajectories is governed by a physical force field.
At each polymorphic site, the attention directed toward a reference lineage acts as an attractive force, pulling the query sequence toward that lineage in sequence space.
Subtracting the sequence's genome-wide baseline attention produces a zero-drift force field: across the entire chromosome, the positive and negative pulls average to zero.
Under clonal evolution, mutations occur independently at random positions.
The instantaneous forces fluctuate in opposing directions and rapidly cancel out, behaving like thermal Brownian motion.
As a result, the sequence particle remains confined to its home corridor, wandering within tight random-walk bounds.
Recombination breaks this physical equilibrium.
Within an introgressed segment, every shared derived allele exerts a sustained, directional pull toward the donor lineage.
This persistent force imparts momentum to the sequence trajectory, deflecting it away from its home corridor and driving a macroscopic excursion across sequence space.
The physical breakpoints correspond precisely to the boundaries of this force field: the exact genomic coordinates where the external directional force turns on and off.

This physical framework yields distinct geometric signatures across different modes of recombination.
In a single crossover, a sequence begins in its home corridor and switches into a donor lineage for the remainder of the chromosome.
The directional force acts persistently from the breakpoint onward, driving cumulative momentum to a sharp, solitary peak at the crossover junction before descending back to zero at the chromosome terminus.
The resulting profile forms a triangular arch whose vertex pinpoints the breakpoint.
For a double crossover (hereafter referred to as a  cassette), a sequence acquires a localized foreign tract before returning to its home lineage.
The directional force turns on at the tract entrance and turns off at the exit, producing a trapezoidal plateau: a steep upward climb at the first breakpoint, a sustained plateau across the cassette, and an equally steep descent at the second breakpoint.
When the true donor is absent from the dataset---such as an unsampled animal reservoir or an extinct lineage---some existing heuristics invariably misidentify innocent sister taxa as parental donors.
\RhizAeon resolves this failure mode through ghost donor introgression: the sequence experiences a powerful force ejecting it from its home corridor, but the deflection fails to align with any contemporary reference lineage.
By recognizing this uncoupled displacement, \RhizAeon autonomously attributes the event to an unsampled ghost ancestor without framing sister taxa.

Localizing breakpoints combines the multi-scale speed of the force field with the fine-scale precision of phylogenetic likelihood.
In natural genomes, recombination tracts vary by orders of magnitude---from fifty-base-pair micro-casettes to multi-kilobase cassettes.
Fixed sliding windows commonly fail here: narrow windows tend to be overwhelmed by local mutational noise, while wide windows can blur recombination signals generated by short cassettes.
\RhizAeon eliminates this trade-off by scanning cumulative momentum with continuous dyadic wavelet filters across a geometric ladder of scales spanning from fifteen to five hundred base pairs.
Fine-scale wavelets isolate short cassettes, while broad-scale wavelets lock onto larger cassettes without getting distracted by localized rate fluctuations.
The zero-crossings and extrema of these wavelet responses narrow candidate breakpoints sites down from whole chromosomes to a localized window of fifteen to thirty base pairs.
In the second stage, \RhizAeon refines this candidate window to individual nucleotide coordinates using profile likelihood polishing.
Because mutations are separated by variable physical distances, the physical crossover could have occurred anywhere between adjacent informative sites.
Within the candidate window, \RhizAeon slides a trial boundary nucleotide by nucleotide, calculating the phylogenetic likelihood under the two competing parental models: sites upstream of the trial boundary are evaluated under the Home lineage model, while sites downstream are evaluated under the Donor model.
The optimal breakpoint is placed at the exact coordinate that maximizes this joint likelihood.
Furthermore, the curvature of the likelihood surface yields rigorous confidence intervals: when parental lineages share an identical conserved stretch, \RhizAeon reports the full uninformative plateau rather than manufacturing an artificial, single-base prediction.

High-throughput trajectory deconvolution scans chromosomes rapidly, but scientific rigor demands that no recombination event is certified on continuous curves alone.
Before reporting any candidate event, \RhizAeon subjects it to an orthogonal Tier~3 statistical due diligence audit operating directly on raw alignment sites.
First, it enforces a Poisson informative site floor: a tract must contain at least three unambiguous informative sites where the donor allele differs from the home allele and matches the recombinant.
Second, it applies an autapomorphic mutation sieve to eliminate rate-acceleration traps: if the mutations separating the candidate from its home lineage are private singletons absent from every other sequence in the dataset, the tract is recognized as a lineage-specific rate burst or APOBEC shower and is rejected.
Third, \RhizAeon conducts an orthogonal Fisher's exact test on a two-by-two contingency table comparing donor- versus home-supporting sites inside the tract against the flanking genomic background.
The mosaic odds ratio must achieve significance under family-wise error rate control across all possible sequence triplets in the cohort.
Finally, if reciprocal transfers are reported between two lineages (where two sequences appear to donate to each other), the algorithm performs cycle adjudication, evaluating dynamic metric velocity reversal to resolve the directed reticulation graph in favor of the true recombinant lineage.
This multi-layered due diligence guarantees that \RhizAeon maintains strict false positive control even under extreme rate heterogeneity.

In natural populations and disease outbreaks, recombinant lineages rarely circulate as isolated singletons.
An ancestral recombination event frequently expands into an epidemic transmission swarm, producing dozens or hundreds of descendant isolates that inherit the identical mosaic tract.
Pairwise and triplet heuristics evaluate each isolate independently, inflating event counts five- to ten-fold and obscuring genuine evolutionary dynamics.
\RhizAeon resolves this inflation through graph adjudication: by clustering isolates that share matching parental lineages and concordant breakpoint coordinates, the algorithm consolidates the entire transmission swarm into a single ancestral recombination event linked to all descendant genomes.
