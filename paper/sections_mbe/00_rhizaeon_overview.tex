\section{The RhizAeon Framework: Principles of Operation}
\label{sec:principles_of_operation}

\RhizAeon models recombination through continuous trajectory mechanics and zero-drift force fields in linear time ($\mathcal{O}(N K L)$ for $N$ taxa, alignment length $L$, and $K$ dynamically selected reference sequences called ``landmark'' lineages). By measuring instantaneous sequence affinity against landmark lineages and routing lineage-specific rate variation into an unpolarized ancestral root sink, \RhizAeon turns recombination detection into a trajectory deconvolution problem. Under clonal evolution, site-to-site fluctuations cancel out as a centered Brownian bridge. Recombination breaks this equilibrium and imparts sustained directional momentum, pinpointing breakpoint sites and grouping ``co-recombinant'' descendants of a shared ancestral recombinant into a single event. Scanning along an alignment from $5'$ to $3'$, a non-recombinant genome keeps a stable genetic identity: its closest relatives stay constant and trace parallel paths through sequence space. Recombination disrupts this stability by merging segments from two or more parental lineages. A single crossover has no intrinsic polarity: if a recombinant joins a segment from lineage $A$ with a segment from lineage $B$, sites in the $A$ segment treat $A$ as the recipient background and $B$ as the foreign donor, whereas sites in the $B$ segment reverse those roles. For consistency, we define a sequence's \emph{home} lineage (major parent) as the landmark with the highest cumulative affinity across the alignment, and its \emph{donor} lineage (minor parent) as the landmark toward which affinity shifts within a localized tract. As a scan moves along a mosaic genome, the trajectory follows its home lineage and then shifts sharply toward a donor lineage across the recombined tract. To capture these shifts without building trees or sliding windows, \RhizAeon evaluates the affinity of each query sequence against the landmark panel at every polymorphic site.

To compute site-level affinity, \RhizAeon adapts the cross-attention operator from transformer neural networks into a deterministic, closed-form analytical formula that requires no neural network training or learned parameters. Each query genome is compared against the landmark keys at every polymorphic position. Rather than weighting all nucleotide matches equally, continuous-time Markov substitution probabilities weight each score: sharing a rare derived allele commands strong attention, whereas common transitions command less. To prevent private mutations from causing false matches, the landmark panel includes an unpolarized ancestral root token that absorbs alleles absent from all sampled landmarks. A softmax operator normalizes these scores into a probability distribution summing to one across all landmarks and the root token at each site. A zero-drift force field then converts these site-by-site attention weights into continuous trajectories. At each polymorphic site, attention toward a landmark acts as an attractive force pulling the query toward that landmark in metric space. Subtracting each landmark's alignment-wide mean attention creates a zero-drift force field where positive and negative pulls average to zero across the genome. Under clonal evolution, random mutations generate opposing pulls that cancel out like thermal Brownian motion, keeping the trajectory within its home corridor. Inside a recombined tract, however, shared derived alleles exert a sustained pull toward the donor lineage, driving a macroscopic excursion away from the home corridor. The breakpoints correspond to the exact coordinates where this directional force turns on and off.

Different recombination architectures and donor sampling regimes produce distinct signatures within this force field. In a single-breakpoint crossover, a sequence starts in its home corridor and switches into a donor corridor at the breakpoint for the rest of the alignment. The directional force drives cumulative momentum up to a single peak at the breakpoint before returning to zero at the end of the sequence, forming a triangular arch whose vertex marks the breakpoint. In a double-breakpoint replacement (a cassette), the directional force turns on at the $5'$ breakpoint and turns off at the $3'$ breakpoint, forming a trapezoidal plateau with a steep climb at the first breakpoint, a flat top across the cassette, and a steep drop at the second breakpoint. When the true donor lineage is unsampled, the introgressed tract loses affinity to the home lineage without matching any sampled taxon. Rather than forcing a false match to a contemporary sequence or dropping the signal, \RhizAeon detects the departure from the home lineage and labels the donor as \texttt{"Ghost"}. Investigators can then either retain the call as a basal or unsampled introgression or mask the tract before phylogenetic inference, without falsely implicating sampled background sequences. When a close relative of the unsampled donor is present in the cohort (a surrogate sister lineage), trajectory tracking detects the shared derived alleles inherited from their common ancestor and attributes the transfer to the sampled sister reference (achieving 85.0\%--100.0\% surrogate attribution accuracy across divergence tiers, and 100.0\% at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$; Section~\ref{subsec:specialized_scenarios}).

To localize breakpoints across tracts that span tens to tens of thousands of bases, \RhizAeon pairs multi-scale force-field scanning with exact contingency-table polishing. Fixed sliding windows face a trade-off: narrow windows are sensitive to mutational noise, whereas wide windows blur short tracts. \RhizAeon avoids this trade-off by combining recursive binary segmentation on the Brownian bridge with a bank of matched filters spanning 30 to 800 base pairs. Fine-scale filters capture short micro-conversions, while broad-scale filters and Brownian-bridge splits capture large transfers without distortion from local rate variation. Once the peaks of these multi-scale responses narrow candidate breakpoints to localized intervals, contingency-table polishing refines each boundary to single-nucleotide coordinates. Across each candidate transition zone, \RhizAeon examines the sequence triplet (recombinant, home landmark, and donor landmark) at every polymorphic site where the home and donor differ. Using two-by-two Fisher's exact tests ($\alpha_{\mathrm{split}} = 0.05 / L$), the polisher recursively tests outward extensions and inward splits of the interval, trimming non-recombinant margins and snapping breakpoints to the exact informative sites that maximize donor-versus-home contrast. Where the parents share identical nucleotides across a junction, the polisher reports the exact uninformative interval $[c_{\mathrm{left}}, c_{\mathrm{right}}]$ between bounding informative sites rather than an arbitrary single base.

In heavily recombined cohorts, choosing reference landmarks only once risks including mosaic genomes in the reference panel, which can hide secondary transfers or invert parent and recombinant roles. To prevent landmark contamination without discarding sequences, \RhizAeon uses a three-pass architecture: initial screening and graph adjudication (Pass~1), iterative forward scrubbing (Pass~2), and backward leave-one-in verification (Pass~3). In Pass~2, candidate recombinant tracts are ranked by signal-to-noise ratio and iteratively masked with gap characters in the working alignment. Outside its masked tract, each sequence remains intact and serves as a clean landmark reference. Gap-masked sites exert zero net force ($\Delta F = 0$) in the attention field, preventing artificial trajectory drift across masked intervals while preserving alignment indel distances elsewhere. Once Pass~2 converges to a recombination-free backbone, Pass~3 restores each candidate tract one at a time while keeping all other tracts masked. Re-evaluating and re-polishing each tract in isolation removes interference from neighboring events and detects parent-recombinant polarity inversions—cases where donor $D$ initially appeared to be a recombinant between $R$ and $H$. Whenever treating $R$ as the recombinant yields sharper transition contrast and lower flanking strain, \RhizAeon swaps the polarity, masks the tract in $R$, and restores parent $D$.

Before certifying any candidate event in Pass~3, an orthogonal three-stage audit checks the raw alignment sites, followed by ancestral event consolidation across the cohort. First, a Poisson informative-site floor requires at least three unambiguous informative sites inside the tract where the recombinant matches the donor allele rather than the home allele. Second, if a tract has a high density of mutations separating the query from its home landmark that are absent from all other sampled sequences, \RhizAeon tests their spatial clustering: tight clusters from an unsampled outgroup are labeled as \texttt{"Ghost"} donor introgressions, whereas scattered private mutations are routed into the root sink and rejected. Third, for tracts attributed to a sampled donor, a two-by-two Fisher's exact test compares donor-matching versus home-matching polymorphic site counts inside the tract against the flanking regions (excluding monomorphic sites and sites where home and donor match) under cohort-wide family-wise error control. Together, iterative scrubbing, leave-one-in verification, and raw-site auditing keep false-positive rates low even under severe mutation rate heterogeneity and heterotachy. Finally, while sparse surveys often detect isolated singleton recombinants, viral outbreaks and bacterial clones frequently contain multiple descendants of a single recombinant ancestor. Graph adjudication clusters these co-recombinant sequences whenever they share compatible parents and overlapping breakpoint intervals, consolidating them into a single ancestral event. Because ``home'' and ``donor'' labels become arbitrary when a crossover spans roughly half the alignment, consolidation checks both direct ($\text{Home}_A \approx \text{Home}_B, \text{Donor}_A \approx \text{Donor}_B$ with overlapping tracts) and inverted ($\text{Home}_A \approx \text{Donor}_B, \text{Donor}_A \approx \text{Home}_B$ with complementary breakpoints) polarities so that co-recombinants with swapped major/minor parent labels are grouped into the same ancestral event.
