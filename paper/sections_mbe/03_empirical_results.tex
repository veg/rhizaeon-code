\subsection{Recombination Patterns in Sarbecoviruses}
\label{subsec:sarbecoviruses}

Sarbecoviruses, the subgenus of coronaviruses that includes SARS-CoV and SARS-CoV-2, evolve under frequent homologous recombination within bat and other mammalian reservoirs.
When investigating recombination across these viral cohorts, genomic screens serve two distinct goals: mapping genome-wide profiles to identify hotspots and coldspots, and reconstructing the mosaic anatomies of individual recombinant genomes.
Phylogenetic partition searches, implemented in tools such as GARD, explore discrete tree space to detect topological discordance.
In their foundational screen of sarbecovirus origins, \citet{lytras2022exploring} downsampled their 78-genome cohort to a representative subset of 22 taxa to make phylogenetic breakpoint screening computationally tractable.
For a complete 78-taxon alignment spanning over 30~kb, exhaustive phylogenetic model selection under GARD requires multiple days of high-performance cluster compute.
Continuous trajectory tracking with \RhizAeon eliminates this bottleneck.
By projecting alignments into continuous metric space, \RhizAeon quantifies spatial velocity changes as a continuous breakpoint flux $\Phi_{\mathrm{bp}}(u)$ in $\mathcal{O}(N K L)$ time.
\RhizAeon scanned the primary 78-taxon alignment of \citet{lytras2022exploring} (30,956~bp MSA, 13,160 informative sites, identifying 32 consolidated events) and an expanded 83-taxon cohort incorporating the five Laotian \textit{Rhinolophus} BANAL isolates of \citet{temmam2022bat} (30,965~bp MSA, 13,260 segregating sites, identifying 38 consolidated events) in minutes rather than days.
Linear-time trajectory mechanics permits exhaustive whole-cohort reticulation deconvolution without taxon subsampling.

The genomic flux reveals sharp evolutionary regionalization across both the expanded 83-taxon MSA (analyzed alongside the nested 78-taxon cohort to evaluate the impact of the five Laotian BANAL bat genomes) and Wuhan-Hu-1 reference NC\_045512.2 coordinates (Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}A and Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_sarbeco_coord_conversion}).
Pronounced coldspots occur in genome regions encoding the catalytic core of RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp, 83-MSA nt 13,674--16,468, Ref nt 13,442--16,236) and the heptad-repeat fusion machinery of Spike S2 (83-MSA nt 24,600--25,600, Ref nt 24,283--25,283), where structural constraints may preclude modular sequence exchanges.
Recombination hotspots concentrate at the Spike $5'$ NTD junction (83-MSA 21,825~nt, Ref 21,566~nt) and the codon 313 hinge between the NTD and receptor-binding domain (RBD, 83-MSA 22,764~nt, Ref 22,468~nt).
Additional prominent hotspots mark the RBD $3'$ exit (83-MSA 23,629~nt, Ref 23,324~nt, upstream of the S1/S2 furin site at 83-MSA 23,932~nt, Ref 23,618~nt) and the ORF8/Nucleocapsid boundary (83-MSA 28,810~nt, Ref 27,900~nt).
Evaluated in a matched coordinate frame against the 21 published consensus GARD breakpoints of \citet{lytras2022exploring}, \RhizAeon breakpoints achieve a median deviation of 131.0~nt in Wuhan-Hu-1 reference coordinates on the primary 78-taxon cohort (11/21 within 150~nt, 52.4\% concordance, and 146.0~nt median in 78-MSA coordinates).
In the expanded 83-taxon cohort, the median deviation is 153.0~nt across both Wuhan-Hu-1 reference and 83-taxon MSA coordinates (10/21 within 150~nt, 47.6\% concordance, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_sarbeco_coord_conversion}).
\RhizAeon localized the nsp2 junction within 2~nt across both frames (83-MSA 1,730 vs.\ 1,732~nt, Ref 1,678 vs.\ 1,680~nt) and the Spike $5'$ NTD boundary within 20~nt (83-MSA 21,623 vs.\ 21,643~nt, Ref 21,391 vs.\ 21,411~nt).
It also localized the nsp16 $3'$ boundary within 23~nt (83-MSA 21,407 vs.\ 21,430~nt, Ref 21,175 vs.\ 21,198~nt), the ORF7b/ORF8 junction within 24~nt in MSA coordinates (21~nt in reference coordinates, 83-MSA 28,556 vs.\ 28,580~nt, Ref 27,664 vs.\ 27,685~nt), and the internal RdRp boundary within 30~nt (83-MSA 14,603 vs.\ 14,633~nt, Ref 14,371 vs.\ 14,401~nt).
Analyzing the exhaustive 83-sequence cohort uncovers fine-grained dynamics invisible under downsampling: incorporating Laotian bat BANAL isolates causes peak breakpoint flux in the Spike NTD to surge almost four-fold (from 0.41 to 1.51~$Z$/nt) and reveals a recurrent micro-recombination-event hotspot. This hotspot corresponds functionally to the antigenic supersite loops identified by cryo-EM, where wildlife reservoirs exchange solvent-accessible micro-recombination cassettes to facilitate antibody escape without destabilizing the spike trimer core \citep{mccallum2021n}.

Simultaneously, continuous trajectory tracking reconstructs the mosaic architecture of individual recombinant lineages at single-nucleotide precision (Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}B,C).
In the Spike glycoprotein (3,843~nt alignment, 1,153 variable sites), \RhizAeon pinpointed the known recombination breakpoint site at codon 313 ($Z = 8.23, P = 4.07 \times 10^{-22}$).
Multi-genome sequence manifold tracking clarifies the longstanding reciprocal attribution ambiguity surrounding SARS-CoV-2 and RaTG13 \citep{boni2020evolutionary}.
In early three-way comparisons lacking intermediate wildlife isolates, the discordance between a RaTG13-like NTD and a pangolin-like ACE2-binding motif led to speculation that an immediate ancestor of SARS-CoV-2 arose through a recent recombination event \citep{Li2020-nd,Wrobel2020-tg}.
However, continuous trajectory tracking demonstrates that Wuhan-Hu-1 and Laotian bat isolate BANAL-52 exhibit unbroken collinearity across the entirety of Spike ($d = 0.0556$ in NTD, $d = 0.0617$ in RBD, and $d = 0.0457$ in S2, Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}B).
Unbroken collinearity across Spike confirms that the human-transmissibility-determining ACE2 contact ridge represents an ancestral bat trait circulating naturally in \textit{Rhinolophus} reservoirs across Southeast Asia.
In contrast, \RhizAeon identifies RaTG13 as the recent recombinant: while sharing the ancestral NTD and S2 backbone ($d \approx 0.05$), its divergence spikes more than 2.5-fold specifically within the RBD ($d = 0.1380$, Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}C).
That localized divergence spike reflects the acquisition of an RBD cassette from an unsampled bat lineage, corroborating \citet{boni2020evolutionary}.

Multi-scale scanning also reveals complex recombination mosaics in other sarbecoviruses from wildlife reservoirs.
In Laotian bat isolate BANAL-247, \RhizAeon identified a cluster of three recombination cassettes (Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}B).
While the Spike backbone of BANAL-247 resembles northern Laos \textit{Rhinolophus malayanus} viruses ($>98.5\%$ identity to BANAL-236/103), its NTD harbors two micro-recombination cassettes at 83-MSA nt 21,909--22,011 (Ref 21,647--21,749~nt, $Z = 155.3$) and 83-MSA nt 22,054--22,251 (Ref 21,791--21,983~nt, $Z = 113.1$).
A third, broader cassette spans the NTD/RBD boundary (83-MSA nt 22,318--22,592, Ref 22,044--22,297~nt, $Z = 81.2$), with all three segments derived from a Thai \textit{Rhinolophus shameli} reservoir closely related to RacCS203 (Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}B,D).
Therefore, \RhizAeon provides empirical support for claims that divergent sarbecoviruses infecting the Southeast Asian bat metapopulations are actively exchanging genetic cassettes by recombination \citep{wacharapluesadee2021evidence}.

\RhizAeon synthesizes these events into a parameterized Ancestral Recombination Graph (ARG, Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}D).
In the Spike glycoprotein, statistical model comparison across 9 sarbecoviruses favors a tripartite modular ARG ($H_3$: NTD chassis, a divergent RBD cassette, and S2 backbone, $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = 0.0$) over unpolarized trees ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = +170.9$) and a clonal null ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = +1654.8$).
Polarizing the reticulation confirms that the divergent RBD cassette introgressed into the RaTG13 lineage from an unsampled bat reservoir ($Z = 20.9, P = 2.1 \times 10^{-9}$).
Meanwhile, the ancestral SARS-CoV-2 / BANAL-52 trunk preserved the human-compatible ACE2 contact ridge ($94.2\%$ synapomorphic concordance, $P = 1.75 \times 10^{-23}$) and reduced residual metric strain by $74.0\%$ ($3.34\% \to 0.87\%$).

Across the 30.9~kb genome of 83 sarbecoviruses, consolidating 38 detected tracts yields an ARG containing 26 unique reticulations (each representing one or more recombination cassettes).
The consolidated network explains $98.82\%$ of total metric strain, matching the $98.85\%$ explained by GARD's 22 unconstrained trees while cutting parameter complexity by $58.5\%$ (1,523 vs.\ 3,674 parameters).
It validates reticulation polarity across 21 polarized events ($79.0\%$ mean in-tract concordance vs.\ $19.0\%$ flanking concordance under the Synapomorphic Site Concordance Ratio, SSCR, $\Delta\mathrm{SSCR} = +0.601$, with $P < 10^{-15}$ across 17/21 events).
Independent validation on a curated 61-taxon sarbecovirus alignment (31,214~bp MSA, 29,926~bp ungapped) under iterative forward scrubbing and backward leave-one-in verification similarly identified 36 verified tracts that consolidate into 34 unique ancestral reticulations.
Across these verified events, mean in-tract concordance reaches $\mathrm{SSCR}_{\mathrm{in}} = 0.813$ compared to $\mathrm{SSCR}_{\mathrm{flank}} = 0.240$ across flanking regions ($\Delta\mathrm{SSCR} = +0.572$), and the screen recovers the canonical Spike glycoprotein mosaic in \texttt{Wuhan-Hu-1} (nt 21,312--24,791, $\mathrm{Home} = \texttt{RmYN02}$, $\mathrm{Donor} = \texttt{MT072865.1}$, $P = 3.35 \times 10^{-262}, Z = 37.78$, $\mathrm{SSCR}_{\mathrm{in}} = 0.739$ vs.\ $\mathrm{SSCR}_{\mathrm{flank}} = 0.112$).

\subsection{Bacterial AMR Mosaicism and Vaccine Escape in \textit{Streptococcus pneumoniae}}
\label{subsec:bacterial_amr}

Bacterial evolution is accelerated by natural transformation and homologous recombination---together referred to hereafter as horizontal gene transfer (HGT)---which can drive the acquisition of antimicrobial resistance (AMR), capsular serotype switching, and antigenic diversification from commensal reservoirs \citep{didelot2010impact,croucher2011rapid}.
In naturally competent pathogens such as \textit{Streptococcus pneumoniae}, transformation enables HGT of genome tracts ranging in size from tens to tens of thousands of base pairs across species boundaries (e.g., from viridans streptococci \textit{Streptococcus mitis} and \textit{Streptococcus oralis}).
Resolving these events across bacterial cohorts has historically relied on recombination inference tools such as ClonalFrame \citep{didelot2007inference}, ClonalFrameML \citep{didelot2015clonalframeml}, and Gubbins \citep{croucher2015rapid}.
These methods perform iterative phylogenetic reconstruction and maximum-likelihood tree peeling, demanding hours to weeks of compute even for modest alignments of a few dozen genomes.

We evaluated \RhizAeon on the complete 2.22~Mb chromosome of the pandemic multidrug-resistant Spain23F-1 lineage of \textit{S.~pneumoniae} (PMEN1, ATCC 700669, 11 representative global isolates spanning 1984--2008, \citealt{croucher2011rapid}).
Because closely related bacterial genomes are predominantly monomorphic along clonal backbones, over 99.9\% of alignment columns carry zero phylogenetic information.
\RhizAeon exploits this sparsity through lossless SNP compression.
Indexing out invariant positions reduces the 2.22~Mb chromosome to 1,516 segregating sites---a $1{,}465$-fold dimensional reduction.
Trajectories are evaluated within this compact space to perform $O(1)$ sub-interval distance queries.
Identified recombination breakpoints map bijectively back to physical base-pair coordinates to project continuous flux $\Phi_{\mathrm{bp}}(u)$ and cumulative strain $\rho(u)$ onto the reference genome.
Six prominent recombination hotspots emerge (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}A),
corresponding to key clinical determinants: the penicillin-binding protein and capsule island ($pbp2x/cps$, 285--352~kb), the trimethoprim resistance island (\textit{folA}, 1,620--1,649~kb), and the surface adhesin cluster (\textit{pspC/cbpA}, 2,105--2,138~kb).

Without constructing discrete trees, continuous changepoints show tight spatial concordance with Gubbins-detected recombination breakpoint sites inferred through iterative RAxML optimization \citep{croucher2011rapid,croucher2015rapid} (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}A).
Across all 52 published Gubbins-inferred whole-chromosome recombination breakpoint sites, \RhizAeon achieves 96.2\% concordance within 2~kb (50/52) and 80.8\% within 1~kb (42/52), with a median spatial deviation of only 92.5~nt.
On primary clinical resistance loci, recombination breakpoints match reference boundaries at single-nucleotide precision: the $5'$ boundary of \textit{pbp2x} and the \textit{pspC} locus were both localized within 1~nt of those identified by Gubbins, while \textit{folA} was identified within 19~nt.

Simultaneously, multi-scale scanning reconstructs the layered mosaic anatomy of resistance and vaccine escape across individual isolates within the 270--370~kb recombination hotspot (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}B).
In isolate 1731, trajectory deconvolution identifies a focal 20.7~kb introgression across \textit{pbp2x} ($Z = 5.35$).
Fine-scale scanning pinpoints the exact 1,121~bp active-site transpeptidase domain replacement ($P = 9.85 \times 10^{-35}, Z = 14.40$) imported from commensal viridans streptococci to abolish $\beta$-lactam binding. Horizontally acquired amino acid substitutions within this catalytic domain sterically remodel the active-site pocket to reduce acylation by penicillins and cephalosporins \citep{dessen2001crystal}.
In isolate 2682, a large 73.9~kb recombination-cassette ($Z = 6.42$) co-transferred \textit{pbp2x} resistance and the capsular locus in a single HGT event (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}B).
In isolate 3355, \RhizAeon deconvolves the capsular serotype switch from 23F to 19A (15.5~kb, $Z = 3.19$) that enabled escape from the 7-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV7), accompanied by a linked low-affinity \textit{pbp1a} allele.
A two-partition tanglegram corroborates this incongruence (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}C): whereas all eleven isolates form a monophyletic complex across the core chromosome, isolates 1731 and 2682 translocate into the divergent commensal viridans cluster (\textit{S.~mitis} / \textit{S.~oralis}, $\Delta d > 6\%$) within the \textit{pbp2x} catalytic domain.
Broad genomic surveys across the genus confirm that co-colonizing oral commensals act as persistent genetic reservoirs, donating divergent mosaic resistance alleles to \textit{S.~pneumoniae} via extensive interspecies gene flow \citep{hanage2009hyper}.

\RhizAeon synthesizes these transfers into a polarized ARG for PMEN1 clinical adaptation (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}D).
Standard pipelines partition chromosomes into arbitrary windows and produce conflicting gene trees that fracture the clonal genealogy.
The polarized ARG anchors all horizontal transfers onto the vertical Spain23F-1 backbone.
It parameterizes the focal \textit{pbp2x} catalytic replacement in 1731, the 73.9~kb mega-cassette in 2682, and the 19A capsular replacement in 3355.
On the 2.22~Mb PMEN1 cohort, full-chromosome deconvolution under iterative phylogenetic tools such as Gubbins required $\sim 14.5$~hours of multicore compute to infer breakpoint sites across successive tree iterations.
In contrast, \RhizAeon completes whole-chromosome metric projection, trajectory tracking, and changepoint detection in 104~ms of core scan time, and finishes the complete end-to-end deconvolution pipeline across the 2.22~Mb chromosome in 42.6~seconds.
Sub-minute chromosome deconvolution achieves an over 1,200-fold end-to-end wall-time acceleration over iterative tree peeling and enables real-time genomic surveillance of multidrug resistance and vaccine escape across bacterial pathogens.

\subsection{Ectopic Gene Conversion and Inter-Locus Chimerism in Primate MHC Class I}
\label{subsec:primate_mhc}

Primate major histocompatibility complex (MHC) Class~I genes (\textit{HLA-A}, \textit{HLA-B}, \textit{HLA-C}) exemplify pathogen-driven balancing selection in vertebrate genomes \citep{parham1995origins,adams2001species}.
Immunogenetic studies reveal a striking structural contrast.
The antigen recognition site (ARS, Exons 2--3, encoding the $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ peptide groove) undergoes rapid diversification through balancing selection and ectopic gene conversion between paralogs.
In contrast, the adjacent $\alpha_3$ structural stalk (Exon 4) remains conserved to preserve invariant contacts with $\beta_2$-microglobulin ($\beta_2$m) and the CD8 co-receptor.


\RhizAeon's Continuous trajectory tracking captures this structural compartmentalization across the full coding sequence without window slicing (Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}A).
We evaluated \RhizAeon on an exhaustive cohort of $N = 379$ Hominoidea classical MHC Class~I alleles ($L = 1{,}128$~bp, 526 segregating polymorphic sites across human, chimpanzee, gorilla, and orangutan, \citealt{maccari2017ipd}).
In 7.89~seconds across all 379 alleles (and 26.5~ms across canonical 8-taxon reference cohorts), \RhizAeon's inferred  breakpoint flux pinpoints the physical boundary between the $\alpha_2$ ARS groove and the $\alpha_3$ structural stalk at nt 608--615 (canonical exon junction 618~bp, Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}A).
Quantitative profiling confirms marked domain segregation: $93.7\%$ of all detected reticulation strain concentrates within the ARS groove (Exons 2--3) and transmembrane anchor (Exon 5), while Exon 4 forms a conserved recombination coldspot that recovers the true paralogous gene tree.

Multi-scale scanning reconstructs the fine mosaic anatomy of hominoid chimeras at single-nucleotide precision (Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}B).
A clinically prominent recombinant is \textit{HLA-B*46:01}, an allele common in Southeast Asia ($>20\%$) associated with myasthenia gravis, severe SARS-CoV-1 infection, and protection from leprosy \citep{hawkins1984strong,lin2003association,hilton2017intergenic}.
\RhizAeon deconvolves \textit{HLA-B*46:01} as an ectopic chimera created by an exact 73~bp micro-conversion recombination event (nt 220--293, codons 66--76, $Z = 2.69, P = 1.15 \times 10^{-7}$) donated by \textit{HLA-C*01:02} into an ancestral \textit{HLA-B*15:01} backbone (Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}B).
The 73~bp tract transfers the C1 functional epitope (Val76-Arg79-Leu80-Arg81) and confers recognition by natural killer (NK) cell inhibitory receptors KIR2DL2/3 absent in all other classical \textit{HLA-B} alleles \citep{barber1996inter}.
High-resolution mass spectrometry confirms that this 73~bp recombination event reshapes the peptide-binding groove, creating a distinctive, low-diversity immunopeptidome that differs substantially from both parental alleles \citep{hilton2017intergenic}.
In non-human hominoids, \RhizAeon characterizes multi-way recombination: in chimpanzee \textit{Patr-AL*01:01}, it identifies a multi-segment recombinant combining \textit{Patr-A} with an ancient \textit{Patr-C} cassette ($Z = 4.03$), while in gorilla \textit{Gogo-C*02:01}, it localizes an ectopic transmembrane exchange at nt 971--1,128.

Segmental phylogenies and a two-partition tanglegram confirm this discordance across the structural boundary (Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}C).
In the Exon 4 $\alpha_3$ stalk (nt 618--894, Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}C1), the neighbor-joining tree reconstructs the ancient locus duplication: \textit{HLA-A}, \textit{HLA-B}, and \textit{HLA-C} form monophyletic clades, with \textit{HLA-B*46:01} clustering sister to \textit{HLA-B*15:01} with zero divergence ($d = 0.000$).
Within the Exon 2 C1 cassette (nt 210--310, Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}C2), \textit{HLA-B*46:01} crosses the locus divide to cluster inside the \textit{HLA-C} clade sister to donor \textit{HLA-C*01:02} ($d = 0.000$).
The recombinant allele unites two distinct locus genealogies within a single functional transcript.

Finally, these polarized transfers can be synthesized into a summary ARG for primate MHC evolution (Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}D), anchoring ectopic gene conversions onto the vertical tandem duplication backbone (ancient divergence of Locus \textit{A}, followed by duplication of \textit{B} and \textit{C}) with directed reticulation arcs for both the 73~bp micro-conversion into \textit{HLA-B*46:01} and deep inter-locus introgression into chimpanzee \textit{Patr-AL*01}.
Directly out of the box, both the command-line tool and the WebAssembly browser GUI output the complete consolidated ancestral reticulation roster (JSON/HTML) together with interactive genome-wide recombination hotspot/coldspot flux profiles ($\Phi_{\mathrm{bp}}(u)$ and $\rho(u)$), per-isolate mosaic architecture tracks, 2D metric manifold projections, and side-by-side midpoint-rooted Neighbor-Joining trees comparing the non-recombinant backbone against any selected recombinant tract.
While parsimony or split decomposition networks omit directional polarity and scale combinatorially, \RhizAeon completes whole-gene trajectory deconvolution across 379 primate alleles in 7.89~seconds, providing the polarized donor-to-recipient transfer edges and partitioned trees needed to resolve gene duplication and ectopic conversion across multigene families.
