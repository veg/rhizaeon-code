\subsection{Deconvolving Recombination Landscapes and Mosaic Genomes in Sarbecoviruses}
\label{subsec:sarbecoviruses}

Sarbecoviruses, the subgenus of coronaviruses that includes SARS-CoV and SARS-CoV-2, evolve under frequent homologous recombination within bat and mammalian reservoirs.
When investigating recombination across these viral cohorts, genomic screens serve two distinct goals: mapping chromosome-wide landscapes to identify hotspots and coldspots, and resolving the mosaic anatomy of individual recombinant genomes.
Phylogenetic partition search, implemented in tools such as GARD, explores discrete tree space to detect topological discordance.
This combinatorial search creates a severe computational bottleneck.
In their screen of sarbecovirus origins, \citet{lytras2022exploring} downsampled a 78-genome cohort to 18 taxa, requiring 4.5~days on a compute cluster to identify 21 breakpoints.
\RhizAeon removes this bottleneck.
By projecting alignments into continuous metric space, it quantifies spatial velocity changes as a continuous breakpoint flux $\Phi_{\mathrm{bp}}(u)$ in $O(N \cdot L)$ time.
\RhizAeon scanned the full 78-taxon alignment (30,956~bp, 13,160 informative sites) in 18.2~seconds and an expanded 83-taxon cohort in 25.0~seconds.
The analysis represents a 20,000-fold speedup over cluster-based phylogenetic inference without taxon subsampling.

The genomic flux reveals sharp evolutionary regionalization: pronounced coldspots protect the catalytic core of RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp, nt 13,400--16,200) and the heptad-repeat fusion machinery of Spike S2 (nt 24,600--25,600).
Structural constraints in these regions preclude modular exchange (Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}A).
Recombination hotspots concentrate at the Spike $5'$ NTD junction (21,825~bp), the codon 313 hinge between the NTD and receptor-binding domain (RBD, 22,764~bp), the S1/S2 furin cleavage site (23,618~bp), and the ORF8/Nucleocapsid boundary (28,810~bp).
Continuous changepoints show close spatial concordance with the 21 published GARD breakpoints of \citet{lytras2022exploring} (median deviation 99.0~nt; Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}A).
\RhizAeon localized the critical S1/S2 junction within 9~nt (24,461 vs.\ 24,452~bp) and the RdRp exit within 12~nt.
Analyzing the exhaustive 83-sequence cohort reveals fine-grained dynamics invisible under downsampling: incorporating Laotian bat BANAL isolates causes peak breakpoint flux in the Spike NTD to surge four-fold (from 0.41 to 1.51~$Z$/nt), identifying a recurrent micro-conversion hub.

Simultaneously, \RhizAeon resolves the mosaic anatomy of individual recombinant lineages at single-nucleotide precision (Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}B,C).
In the Spike glycoprotein (3,843~nt, 1,153 variable sites; scanned in 17.8~ms), \RhizAeon pinpointed the historic crossover in SARS-CoV-2 (Wuhan-Hu-1) at codon 313 ($Z = 8.23, P = 4.07 \times 10^{-22}$).
Rather than treating Wuhan-Hu-1 as an ancestral baseline, \RhizAeon deconvolves Wuhan-Hu-1 as an emergent chimera assembled from discrete wildlife cassettes (Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}B).
Upstream of codon 313, Wuhan-Hu-1 clusters with Yunnan bat isolate RaTG13 (94.4\% identity, $d = 0.0556$).
Downstream in the RBD, it shifts affinity toward pangolin MP789 and Laotian bat isolate BANAL-52 ($d = 0.0805$), acquiring the human-adapted ACE2 contact ridge.
Partitioned neighbor-joining trees and a two-partition tanglegram corroborate this topological discordance (Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}C).
Wuhan-Hu-1 clusters sister to RaTG13 in the Spike NTD (codons 1--313; Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}C1).
In the RBD (codons 314--540; Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}C2), RaTG13 diverges basally, while BANAL-52 and pangolin MP789 group sister to Wuhan-Hu-1.

Multi-scale scanning also resolves mosaic architectures in wildlife reservoirs.
In Laotian bat isolate BANAL-247, \RhizAeon identified a tripartite mosaic (Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}B).
While its backbone and S2 subunit belong to northern Laos \textit{Rhinolophus malayanus} viruses ($>98.5\%$ identity to BANAL-236/103), its Spike NTD harbors three nested micro-conversions (nt 21,909--22,011, $Z = 155.3$; nt 22,054--22,251, $Z = 113.1$; nt 22,318--22,592, $Z = 81.2$).
These were donated by a Thai \textit{Rhinolophus shameli} reservoir related to RacCS203 (Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}B,D).
Cross-clade exchange demonstrates that Southeast Asian sarbecoviruses undergo modular gene transfer across divergent host species and regions.

\RhizAeon synthesizes these events into a parameterized Ancestral Recombination Graph (ARG; Figure~\ref{fig:sarbecovirus_recombination_landscape}D).
In the Spike glycoprotein, statistical model comparison across 9 sarbecoviruses decisively favors a tripartite modular ARG ($H_3$: NTD chassis, ACE2 receptor ridge, and S2 backbone; $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = 0.0$) over unpolarized trees ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = +170.9$) and a clonal null ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = +1654.8$).
Polarizing the Wuhan-Hu-1 reticulation demonstrates that the human-transmissible receptor ridge was introgressed into an ancestral bat RaTG13-like chassis ($94.2\%$ in-tract synapomorphic concordance vs.\ $62.1\%$ flanking, $P = 1.75 \times 10^{-23}$), reducing residual metric strain by $74.0\%$ ($3.34\% \to 0.87\%$).
Across the 30.9~kb genome of 83 sarbecoviruses, consolidating 45 detected tracts yields an ARG of 44 unique reticulations.
The consolidated network explains $98.82\%$ of total metric strain, matching the $98.85\%$ explained by GARD's 22 unconstrained trees while cutting parameter complexity by $58\%$ (1,559 vs.\ 3,674 parameters).
It validates reticulation polarity across 38 events ($81.8\%$ mean in-tract concordance, $P < 10^{-15}$).

\subsection{Bacterial AMR Mosaicism and Vaccine Escape in \textit{Streptococcus pneumoniae}}
\label{subsec:bacterial_amr}

Bacterial evolution is accelerated by natural transformation and homologous recombination, driving rapid antimicrobial resistance (AMR), capsular serotype switching, and antigenic diversification from commensal reservoirs \citep{didelot2010impact,croucher2011rapid}.
In naturally competent pathogens such as \textit{Streptococcus pneumoniae}, horizontal gene transfer imports replacement tracts ranging from tens of base pairs to over 70~kilobases across species boundaries (e.g., from viridans streptococci \textit{Streptococcus mitis} and \textit{Streptococcus oralis}).
Resolving these events across bacterial cohorts has historically required heavy computation: ancestral inference tools like ClonalFrame \citep{didelot2007inference} and Gubbins \citep{croucher2015rapid} rely on iterative phylogenetic reconstruction and tree peeling via maximum likelihood, demanding hours to weeks of compute.

\RhizAeon bypasses iterative tree reconstruction by tracking whole-chromosome sequence trajectories in continuous metric space, quantifying spatial turbulence as breakpoint flux $\Phi_{\mathrm{bp}}(u)$ (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}A).
We evaluated \RhizAeon on the complete 2.22~Mb chromosome of the pandemic multidrug-resistant Spain23F-1 lineage of \textit{S.~pneumoniae} (PMEN1, ATCC 700669; 11 representative global isolates spanning 1984--2008; \citealt{croucher2011rapid}).
Because closely related bacterial genomes are predominantly monomorphic along clonal backbones, over 99.9\% of alignment columns carry zero phylogenetic information.
\RhizAeon exploits this sparsity through lossless SNP compression.
Indexing out invariant positions reduces the 2.22~Mb chromosome to 1,516 segregating sites---a $1{,}465$-fold dimensional reduction.
Trajectories are evaluated within this compact space to perform $O(1)$ sub-interval distance queries.
Identified changepoints map bijectively back to physical base-pair coordinates, projecting continuous flux $\Phi_{\mathrm{bp}}(u)$ and cumulative strain $\rho(u)$ onto the reference genome.
Six prominent recombination hotspots emerge (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}A).
The identified hotspots correspond to key clinical determinants: the penicillin-binding protein and capsule island ($pbp2x/cps$, 285--352~kb), the trimethoprim resistance island (\textit{folA}, 1,620--1,649~kb), and the surface adhesin cluster (\textit{pspC/cbpA}, 2,105--2,138~kb).

Without constructing discrete trees, continuous changepoints show tight spatial concordance with Gubbins boundaries inferred through iterative RAxML optimization \citep{croucher2011rapid,croucher2015rapid} (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}A).
Across all 52 published whole-chromosome recombination boundaries, \RhizAeon achieves 96.2\% concordance within 2~kb (50/52) and 80.8\% within 1~kb, with a median spatial deviation of only 92.5~nt.
On primary clinical resistance loci, changepoints match reference boundaries at single-nucleotide precision: the $5'$ boundary of \textit{pbp2x} and the \textit{pspC} locus were both localized within 1~nt of Gubbins, while \textit{folA} was identified within 19~nt.
Metric trajectory tracking faithfully recovers the genomic footprint of bacterial transformation without iterative tree search.

Simultaneously, multi-scale scanning resolves the layered mosaic anatomy of resistance and vaccine escape across individual isolates within the 270--370~kb hotspot (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}B).
In isolate 1731, \RhizAeon resolves a focal 20.7~kb introgression across \textit{pbp2x} ($Z = 5.35$).
Fine-scale scanning pinpoints the exact 1,121~bp active-site transpeptidase domain replacement ($P = 9.85 \times 10^{-35}, Z = 14.40$) imported from commensal viridans streptococci to abolish $\beta$-lactam binding.
In isolate 2682, a massive 73.9~kb mega-cassette ($Z = 6.42$) co-transferred \textit{pbp2x} resistance and the capsular locus in a single transformation event (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}B).
In isolate 3355, \RhizAeon deconvolves the capsular serotype switch from 23F to 19A (15.5~kb, $Z = 3.19$) that enabled escape from the 7-valent conjugate vaccine (PCV7), accompanied by a linked low-affinity \textit{pbp1a} allele.
A two-partition tanglegram corroborates this incongruence (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}C): whereas all 11 isolates form a monophyletic complex across the core chromosome, isolates 1731 and 2682 translocate into the divergent commensal viridans cluster (\textit{S.~mitis} / \textit{S.~oralis}, $\Delta d > 6\%$) within the \textit{pbp2x} catalytic domain.

\RhizAeon synthesizes these transfers into a polarized ARG for PMEN1 clinical adaptation (Figure~\ref{fig:spneumoniae_chromosome_landscape}D).
Standard pipelines partition chromosomes into arbitrary windows, producing conflicting gene trees that fracture the clonal genealogy.
The polarized ARG anchors all horizontal transfers onto the vertical Spain23F-1 backbone.
It parameterizes the focal \textit{pbp2x} catalytic replacement in 1731, the 73.9~kb mega-cassette in 2682, and the 19A capsular replacement in 3355.
On the 2.22~Mb PMEN1 cohort, ClonalFrame required 705~hours ($\sim 1$~month) of MCMC sampling, and Gubbins required 1.5~hours (5,400~seconds) of iterative tree peeling.
\RhizAeon completes whole-chromosome metric projection, trajectory tracking, and changepoint detection in 114~ms (127~ms in native Rust).
The method achieves an over 30,000-fold speedup over Gubbins and an 18,000,000-fold speedup over ClonalFrame.
Rapid execution enables real-time surveillance of multidrug resistance and vaccine escape across bacterial pathogens.

\subsection{Ectopic Gene Conversion and Inter-Locus Chimerism in Primate MHC Class I}
\label{subsec:primate_mhc}

Primate major histocompatibility complex (MHC) Class~I genes (\textit{HLA-A}, \textit{HLA-B}, \textit{HLA-C}) exemplify pathogen-driven balancing selection in vertebrate genomes \citep{parham1995origins,adams2001species}.
Immunogenetic studies reveal a striking structural contrast.
The antigen recognition site (ARS; Exons 2--3, encoding the $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ peptide groove) undergoes rapid diversification through balancing selection and ectopic gene conversion between paralogs.
In contrast, the adjacent $\alpha_3$ structural stalk (Exon 4) remains conserved to preserve invariant contacts with $\beta_2$-microglobulin ($\beta_2$m) and the CD8 co-receptor.
Phylogenetic methods struggle with this modular architecture: full-length alignments generate topological conflicts that collapse locus monophyly, whereas sliding windows require arbitrary boundaries and risk missing short micro-conversions.

\RhizAeon resolves this structural compartmentalization by tracking continuous trajectories across the full coding sequence without window slicing (Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}A).
We evaluated \RhizAeon on an exhaustive cohort of $N = 379$ Hominoidea classical MHC Class~I alleles ($L = 1{,}128$~bp, 526 segregating polymorphic sites across human, chimpanzee, gorilla, and orangutan; \citealt{maccari2017ipd}).
In 16.34~seconds across all 379 alleles (and 3.59~ms across canonical 8-taxon reference cohorts), continuous trajectory tracking produces breakpoint flux $\Phi_{\mathrm{bp}}(u)$ and cumulative strain $\rho(u)$.
Without structural annotations, the breakpoint flux pinpoints the physical boundary between the $\alpha_2$ ARS groove and the $\alpha_3$ structural stalk at nt 608--615 (canonical exon junction 618~bp; Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}A).
Quantitative profiling confirms marked domain segregation: $93.7\%$ of all detected reticulation strain concentrates within the ARS groove (Exons 2--3) and transmembrane anchor (Exon 5), while Exon 4 forms a conserved coldspot that recovers the true paralogous gene tree.

Multi-scale scanning resolves the fine mosaic anatomy of hominoid chimeras at single-nucleotide precision (Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}B).
A clinically prominent recombinant is \textit{HLA-B*46:01}, an allele common in Southeast Asia ($>20\%$) associated with nasopharyngeal carcinoma, myasthenia gravis, and severe SARS-CoV-1 infection.
\RhizAeon deconvolves \textit{HLA-B*46:01} as an ectopic chimera created by an exact 73~bp micro-conversion (nt 220--293, codons 66--76; $Z = 2.69, P = 1.15 \times 10^{-7}$) donated by \textit{HLA-C*01:02} into an ancestral \textit{HLA-B*15:01} backbone (Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}B).
The 73~bp tract transfers the C1 functional epitope (Val76-Arg79-Leu80-Arg81), conferring recognition by natural killer (NK) cell inhibitory receptors KIR2DL2/3 absent in all other classical \textit{HLA-B} alleles \citep{barber1996inter}.
In non-human hominoids, \RhizAeon resolves multi-way chimerism: in chimpanzee \textit{Patr-AL*01:01}, it identifies a multi-segment recombinant combining \textit{Patr-A} with an ancient \textit{Patr-C} cassette ($Z = 4.03$), while in gorilla \textit{Gogo-C*02:01}, it localizes an ectopic transmembrane exchange at nt 971--1,128.

Segmental phylogenies and a two-partition tanglegram confirm this discordance across the structural boundary (Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}C).
In the Exon 4 $\alpha_3$ stalk (nt 618--894; Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}C1), the neighbor-joining tree cleanly reconstructs the ancient locus duplication: \textit{HLA-A}, \textit{HLA-B}, and \textit{HLA-C} form monophyletic clades, with \textit{HLA-B*46:01} clustering sister to \textit{HLA-B*15:01} with zero divergence ($d = 0.000$).
Within the Exon 2 C1 cassette (nt 210--310; Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}C2), \textit{HLA-B*46:01} crosses the locus divide to cluster inside the \textit{HLA-C} clade sister to donor \textit{HLA-C*01:02} ($d = 0.000$).
The recombinant allele unites two distinct locus genealogies within a single functional transcript.

Finally, \RhizAeon integrates these transfers into a polarized ARG for primate MHC evolution (Figure~\ref{fig:hominoidea_mhc_landscape}D).
The polarized ARG anchors ectopic gene conversions onto the vertical tandem duplication backbone (ancient divergence of Locus \textit{A}, followed by duplication of \textit{B} and \textit{C}).
Directed reticulation arcs parameterize both the 73~bp micro-conversion into \textit{HLA-B*46:01} and deep inter-locus introgression into chimpanzee \textit{Patr-AL*01}.
While parsimony or split decomposition networks omit directional polarity and scale combinatorially, \RhizAeon completes whole-gene trajectory deconvolution across 379 primate alleles in 16.34~seconds.
By unifying metric trajectory tracking with polarized ARG construction, \RhizAeon provides a scalable engine for resolving gene duplication and ectopic conversion across multigene families.
