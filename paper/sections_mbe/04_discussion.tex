\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Recombination is both a major driver of genome evolution and a serious confounder of phylogenetic analysis. When recombination splices sequence tracts with different evolutionary histories into a single genome, no single bifurcating tree can accurately represent that genome's relationships to others. Detecting and characterizing recombination events is therefore an essential step in molecular evolution pipelines. \RhizAeon advances this step by embedding sequence alignments into continuous metric space, where recombination breakpoints and mosaic segments emerge as physical trajectory shifts without losing their broader taxonomic context. Velocity shifts along \RhizAeon trajectories pinpoint local genealogical discordances at breakpoint sites, while ancestral consolidation groups sequences that share breakpoints and parental lineages into co-recombinant clades. Continuous trajectory tracking thus bridges fine-scale population-genetic ARG inference and macro-evolutionary phylogenetic networks.

Natural viral and bacterial populations routinely violate standard neutral coalescent assumptions: outbreaks produce dense clusters of nearly identical descendants, bacterial HGT imports short DNA tracts into megabase chromosomes, and donor lineages often remain unsampled in wild host reservoirs. Our simulation benchmarks and empirical analyses show that \RhizAeon handles each of these challenges. In epidemic clusters, bidirectional trajectory consolidation collapses co-recombinant clades into single ancestral events, preventing event-count inflation across descendants of a shared recombinant ancestor. In complex mosaic genomes with three or more parents, continuous directional projection separates multiple recombination cassettes from different donors within the same sequence. When introgressed tracts come from unsampled lineages that branched basal to the alignment MRCA, \RhizAeon's orphan-divergence audit flags the donor as \texttt{"Ghost"}—allowing investigators to identify or mask basal introgressions without falsely implicating sampled taxa—while attributing transfers to a sampled surrogate sister lineage whenever one is available. Across bacterial chromosomes, multi-scale matched filters isolate short micro-conversions without relying on fixed sliding windows.

Sub-second to multi-second runtimes also change the practical economics of recombination screening in genomic surveillance. In addition to running faster and scaling better than exact triplet scanners such as 3SEQ and RDP5, \RhizAeon compiles to WebAssembly to run directly inside standard web browsers. Public health laboratories can therefore screen pathogen cohorts locally on any computer without uploading sensitive sequence data to external servers or installing software dependencies. These rapid whole-genome screens can directly inform outbreak preparedness and vaccine design. In sarbecoviruses, \RhizAeon resolved the debate over whether RaTG13 or SARS-CoV-2 recently acquired a recombinant Spike RBD: unbroken trajectory collinearity confirmed that the human-compatible ACE2 contact ridge was already present in the common ancestor of SARS-CoV-2 and Laotian BANAL bat viruses, whereas RaTG13 acquired a divergent RBD cassette from an unsampled bat virus reservoir. In \textit{Streptococcus pneumoniae}, whole-chromosome trajectory tracking pinpointed the 23F $\to$ 19A capsular switch that drives clinical escape from the PCV7 conjugate vaccine alongside \textit{pbp2x} penicillin-resistance replacements, enabling real-time tracking of adaptive antigenic and resistance shifts.

Several biological boundary conditions merit consideration. First, \RhizAeon requires enough mutations inside a transferred tract to distinguish recombination from random substitution noise; in very low-divergence cohorts ($d < 0.005$), sparse mutations set an intrinsic physical limit for all sequence-based recombination detectors. Second, although trajectory mechanics localizes breakpoints and assigns parental clades accurately, fine-scale branch-length estimation within partitioned segments still benefits from downstream maximum-likelihood or Bayesian phylogenetic inference. Looking ahead, continuous trajectory tracking provides a natural bridge to sequence foundation models: coupling attention matrices from pre-trained protein language models with \RhizAeon's metric mechanics can annotate recombined tracts both structurally and functionally as they emerge. Replacing discrete tree searches with continuous sequence kinematics thus provides a fast, scalable foundation for characterizing recombination across modern genomic pipelines.
