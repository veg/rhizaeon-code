\subsection{Coalescent ARG Benchmark and Calibration Against 3SEQ}
\label{subsec:posada_benchmark}

To establish a baseline against 3SEQ---evaluated here as a fast, widely used representative of exact triplet-scanning algorithms rather than all triplet scanners---we benchmarked \RhizAeon across the 3,200 coalescent simulation scenarios of \citet{posada2001evaluation} ($N = 10$ taxa, $L = 1{,}000$~nt, Figure~\ref{fig:posada_benchmark}, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_posada_summary}).
Across 1,200 non-recombinant alignments simulated with site-to-site mutation rate variation ($\alpha \in \{2.0, 0.5, 0.05\}$), both algorithms maintained strict false positive control well below the nominal 5\% ceiling (Figure~\ref{fig:posada_benchmark}C).
3SEQ exhibited a lower false positive rate on these small 10-taxon recombination-free alignments (0.17\%, 2 false alarms / 1,200 trials, 95\% Wilson score interval [0.05\%, 0.60\%]), whereas \RhizAeon yielded 1.42\% (17 false positives / 1,200 trials, 95\% Wilson score interval [0.89\%, 2.26\%], with 0.0\% false positives across all 300 trials at $\theta = 10$ and 8 of the 12 rate-variation conditions).
Occasional false positive recombination detections occurred primarily at high sequence divergence ($\theta = 200$, where 14 of the 17 false alarms occurred across $\alpha \in \{2.0, 0.5, 0.05\}$) due to stochastic homoplasy across the 1,000~nt locus, rather than concentrating in datasets with extreme site-to-site mutation rate variation.
Notably, because the \citet{posada2001evaluation} protocol models Gamma rate variation strictly across sites rather than heterotachously along individual lineages, 3SEQ remains well-calibrated here; when lineage-specific rate accelerations are introduced (evaluated below in Section~\ref{subsec:grand_sweep_envelope}), private mutations on a single branch lack directional affinity toward candidate donors and route harmlessly into \RhizAeon's root sink.

Across the 1,600 recombinant alignments ($\rho \in \{1, 4, 16, 64\}$), detection power depended primarily on mutational availability (Figure~\ref{fig:posada_benchmark}A).
In low-diversity datasets ($\theta = 10$, fewer than 25 variable sites), sensitivity was strictly limited by mutational density: both \RhizAeon and 3SEQ detected 2.0\%--22.0\% of alignments across $\rho \in [1, 64]$, where frequent recombination events split sequences into short segments carrying few mutations.
At higher mutational densities ($\theta \ge 50$), 3SEQ's more permissive default triplet threshold provided a sensitivity advantage at intermediate recombination rates (e.g., 73.0\% vs.\ 59.0\% at $\theta = 50, \rho = 4$), while both methods converged to 90.0\%--100.0\% power at $\rho \ge 16$ ($\theta \in \{100, 200\}$).
Even on these compact 10-taxon alignments ($N = 10$, where $\binom{10}{3} = 120$ triplets require minimal combinatorial work), \RhizAeon executed in a mean of 12.2--33.5~ms per alignment (10.3--24.4~ms across 31 of 32 conditions, 13.6~ms across all 1,200 rate-variation nulls) compared to 27.6--36.8~ms for 3SEQ; as cohort sizes expand to $N = 32\text{--}55$ taxa in our surveillance benchmarks below, \RhizAeon's $\mathcal{O}(N K L)$ trajectory architecture runs up to 5.5-fold faster than $\mathcal{O}(N^3 L)$ triplet enumeration.

A central distinction between \RhizAeon and raw 3SEQ outputs lies in event cardinality and bipartite event precision (Figure~\ref{fig:posada_benchmark}B and Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_posada_summary}).
Because our Hudson coalescent simulator explicitly records the simulated Ancestral Recombination Graph (ARG) and extracts all observable topological transfers ($K_{\mathrm{true}}$, Section~\ref{supp:bipartite_matching_full}), we directly compared inferred event counts ($\hat{K}$) against both the exact simulated ARG events and Hudson's theoretical expectation ($\mathbb{E}[R]$, dashed line).
When 3SEQ is run without downstream post-processing, it outputs every sequence triplet displaying a significant hypergeometric random-walk excursion.
Even when an alignment contains only a single recombinant sequence, multiple combinations of parent-like proxy sequences in the alignment—compounded by co-recombinant descendants inheriting the same ancestral crossover—cause raw triplet counts to reach $\hat{K} = 16.3\text{--}17.3$ at $\theta = 200, \rho \in \{16, 64\}$.
In contrast, \RhizAeon analyzes all sequences jointly through iterative scrubbing and leave-one-in verification, grouping shared segments into consolidated ancestral events ($\hat{K} \approx 1.49\text{--}5.08$ at $\theta = 200$).
Under strict one-to-one Hungarian bipartite matching weighted by parental triad attribution ($S_{\mathrm{triad}}$) and SNP overlap ($J_{\mathrm{SNP}}$, Equation~\ref{eq:methods_event_affinity}), \RhizAeon achieves 2.4- to 6.1-fold higher continuous event-level precision than unconsolidated 3SEQ across $\theta \ge 50$ ($33.0\%\text{--}60.0\%$ vs.\ $4.6\%\text{--}46.5\%$ at $\theta = 200$).
However, as emphasized in the 3SEQ documentation \citep{lam20183seq}, raw 3SEQ triplet lists are intended to be post-processed via downstream consolidation scripts that deduplicate redundant proxy-parent triplets and group overlapping breakpoint intervals.
We therefore implemented a faithful downstream 3SEQ consolidation pipeline---\texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)}---and evaluated it head-to-head against both raw \texttt{3SEQ} and \RhizAeon across four specialized epidemiological and evolutionary benchmark suites.

\subsection{Performance Across Specialized Surveillance and Evolutionary Regimes}
\label{subsec:specialized_scenarios}

Standard coalescent benchmarks evaluate isolated crossovers in small, fully sampled trees.
In natural populations and disease outbreaks, however, recombination violates these idealized conditions: recombinant lineages expand into co-recombinant transmission clusters, acquire sequence tracts from multiple distinct donors, inherit material from unsampled reservoirs, or exchange short micro-conversion tracts (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}).
To isolate and fairly evaluate (1) breakpoint localization accuracy, (2) recombinant and proxy-parent attribution, and (3) co-recombinant cluster consolidation without conflating raw triplet enumeration with consolidated event inference, we evaluated \RhizAeon alongside both unconsolidated \texttt{3SEQ} and post-processed \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} across 2,280 alignments spanning four specialized simulation regimes.

\paragraph{Epidemic Clonal Expansion}
During viral outbreaks and bacterial epidemics, an ancestral recombinant frequently transmits across multiple hosts and expands into a clade or cluster of $K$ sampled co-recombinant isolates (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}A).
Across 300 simulated outbreak cohorts ($N = 42\text{--}55$ taxa, $L = 5{,}000$~nt, $K \in \{2, 5, 8, 10, 15\}$), unconsolidated \texttt{3SEQ} reported every significant sequence triplet independently, inflating apparent alert counts from $\hat{K} = 9.5\text{--}30.5$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$) to $36.8\text{--}55.0$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$) and flagging background parental lineages as the middle recombinant sequence in reciprocally inverted triplets.
Applying downstream consolidation in \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)}—filtering reciprocal parent inversions via directional transition contrast and clustering overlapping co-recombinant intervals—completely resolved this single-event epidemic regime at moderate-to-high divergence ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$): \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} collapsed the co-recombinant cluster into $\hat{K} = 1.00\text{--}1.10$ unique events with $99.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall, $93.8\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision, $\le 2.5\%$ background false alarms, and $5.6\text{--}14.0$~nt mean breakpoint error ($J = 0.98\text{--}0.99$, Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}A).
\RhizAeon achieved virtually identical consolidation and accuracy directly within its native linear-time pass ($\hat{K} = 1.00\text{--}1.05$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$; $\hat{K} = 1.00$ across all $K$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$), recovering $100.0\%$ of co-recombinant descendants across all $K$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ ($90.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$) with $97.3\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision ($100.0\%$ in 14 of 15 conditions), $0.00\%$ background false positives in 14 of 15 conditions, and $11.0\text{--}16.3$~nt mean breakpoint error at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$ ($J = 0.98\text{--}0.99$), while executing up to 5.5-fold faster than 3SEQ ($0.95\text{--}1.98$~s vs.\ $1.06\text{--}10.87$~s).
At low parental divergence ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$), \RhizAeon recovered a substantially higher fraction of co-recombinant cluster members than \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} as cluster size grew ($90.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall vs.\ $28.0\%\text{--}45.0\%$ for $K \in \{8, 10, 15\}$).

\paragraph{Multi-Way Mosaic Clusters}
Natural viral and bacterial recombinants frequently acquire genetic material from more than one parental donor, forming multi-way mosaic architectures including dual cassettes, nested "Russian doll" transfers, and serial triple crossovers (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}B).
\RhizAeon accommodates multi-parent mosaicism by iteratively scrubbing detected recombinant segments and tracking continuous directional excursions against all landmark reference channels simultaneously, decomposing a single query genome into multiple distinct donor transitions.
In Dual Cassette recombinants ($P_2$ and $P_3$ cassettes embedded within a $P_1$ background), \RhizAeon recovered both parental donors in nearly every trial (averaging $1.98$ of $2.00$ expected donors across all regimes and $1.99$ of $2.00$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$, Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}B), with $100.0\%$ recall across all conditions, $97.5\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision, $\le 1.1\%$ background false alarms, and high spatial boundary overlap ($J = 0.97\text{--}0.98$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$).
In Nested Russian Dolls ($P_1 \to P_2 \to P_3 \to P_2 \to P_1$) and Triple Crossovers ($P_1 \to P_2 \to P_3$), \RhizAeon resolved an average of $1.62$ ($1.40\text{--}1.80$) and $1.06$ ($0.60\text{--}1.40$) donors respectively while maintaining $93.3\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision and $\le 5.0\%$ background false positives.
By contrast, because 3SEQ's exact hypergeometric random-walk scan evaluates one triplet and extracts a single dominant recombinant segment per triplet call, even after downstream consolidation \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} recovered only a single donor per mosaic genome ($1.00\text{--}1.15$ of $2.00$ expected donors in Dual Cassettes and $1.00$ in Nested Dolls, Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}B).
Furthermore, when a single mosaic genome carries tracts from two different donors ($P_2$ and $P_3$), evaluating triplet combinations $(R, P_1, P_2)$ across the $P_3$-derived segment distorts the flanking baseline and causes \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} to suffer $16.2\%\text{--}32.9\%$ background false-positive calls in Dual Cassettes at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ (down from $47.1\%\text{--}81.0\%$ in raw 3SEQ, compared to $0.0\%\text{--}1.1\%$ for \RhizAeon).

\paragraph{Ghost Donor Introgression}
In empirical surveillance, recombination frequently involves donor lineages absent from the analyzed alignment, such as unsampled wildlife reservoirs or extinct ancestral variants (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}C).
In this regime, 3SEQ does not claim to identify the true unsampled parent; rather, it uses sampled sequences in the alignment as proxy parents to detect the localized shift in sequence similarity and localize the breakpoint boundaries.
When a close relative of the unsampled donor is present in the cohort (the Surrogate Sister regime), both \RhizAeon and \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} perform exceptionally well: at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$, \RhizAeon achieves $100.0\%$ recombinant recall, $100.0\%$ precision, $0.0\%$ background and parent false alarms, $J = 0.96\text{--}0.99$ boundary overlap, and attributes the introgressed tract to the sampled surrogate sister clade in $100.0\%$ of trials ($88.3\%\text{--}97.1\%$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$), while \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} achieves $100.0\%$ recall, $88.7\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision, $J = 0.97\text{--}0.99$, and $95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ surrogate sister attribution (vastly improving upon unconsolidated 3SEQ's $9.3\%\text{--}39.3\%$ precision and $37.5\%\text{--}56.4\%$ background false alarms).
However, when the introgressing donor branches basal to the MRCA of the sampled alignment with no close sampled relative (the Pure Ghost and Deep Reservoir regimes), a critical trade-off emerges.
Because 3SEQ triplets lack an outgroup root reference to orient which sequence in the triplet diverged, an introgressed basal ghost tract in recombinant $R$ (within clade $P_1$) makes $R$ appear as a distant outgroup inside the tract while non-recombinant $P_1$ sequences remain closely related to $P_1$ inside the tract and to proxy $P_2$ outside the tract; consequently, both raw \texttt{3SEQ} and \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} accurately localize the breakpoint coordinates ($J = 0.95\text{--}0.99$), but invert the recombinant identity—failing to identify $R$ as the recombinant ($0.0\%$ recall for \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)}) and instead flagging non-recombinant $P_1$ background leaves as the recombinant in $50.0\%$ of background taxa ($100.0\%$ parent false alarm rate).
Conversely, rather than discarding basal introgressions or misattributing them to contemporary proxy clades, \RhizAeon's iterative scrubbing and orphan-divergence detector explicitly flags tracts where a sequence departs sharply from its home clade without matching any sampled contemporary reference and labels the donor as \texttt{"Ghost"} (unsampled lineage).
Across all Pure Ghost cohorts ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} \in \{0.02, 0.05, 0.10\}, K \in \{2, 5, 10\}$), \RhizAeon detects $93.5\%\text{--}100.0\%$ of ghost-introgressed recombinants with $100.0\%$ precision, $J = 0.87\text{--}0.98$ boundary overlap, $95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ \texttt{"Ghost"} donor attribution, and $0.0\%$ background false alarms (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}C).
Similarly, across Deep Reservoir cohorts ($d_{\mathrm{reservoir}} = 0.15$), \RhizAeon achieves $90.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall, $100.0\%$ precision, $J = 0.89\text{--}0.98$ boundary overlap, $90.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ \texttt{"Ghost"} donor attribution, and $0.0\%$ background false alarms at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ ($80.0\%\text{--}95.5\%$ recall and $95.3\%\text{--}96.1\%$ precision at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$).

\paragraph{Bacterial Micro-Conversions}
In bacterial transformation (e.g., \emph{Streptococcus pneumoniae}, \emph{Helicobacter pylori}), homologous replacement frequently involves short micro-conversion tracts spanning $<10\%$ of the locus (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}D).
Detecting micro-conversions is governed by the information-theoretic count of distinguishing polymorphisms within the tract: at $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 50$~nt ($L = 5{,}000$~nt), simulated tracts carried a mean of only $0.8\text{--}1.4$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$), $1.8\text{--}2.9$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.05$), and $4.9\text{--}5.2$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$) mutations.
Because both \RhizAeon's windowless dyadic matched filter and 3SEQ's windowless exact hypergeometric random-walk scan enforce strict family-wise error rate thresholds across all sequence comparisons, tracts carrying fewer than 4--5 informative SNPs ($L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 50$~nt) rarely cross genome-wide significance under either method ($0.0\%\text{--}10.0\%$ recall for \RhizAeon, $0.0\%\text{--}10.5\%$ for raw \texttt{3SEQ}, and $0.0\%\text{--}5.0\%$ for \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)}, with $0.0\%$ false positives).
At $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 100$~nt ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$, $8.0\text{--}10.6$ SNPs), raw \texttt{3SEQ}'s more lenient triplet threshold provides higher raw sensitivity ($45.0\%\text{--}70.5\%$) than \RhizAeon ($20.0\%\text{--}54.5\%$, with $100.0\%$ precision and $0.0\%$ background false alarms), highlighting the classic trade-off between maximizing power (when scrubbing all putative recombination signals from an alignment) and enforcing conservative specificity (when certifying individual recombination events).
As tract lengths reach $L_{\mathrm{tract}} \in \{200, 400, 800\}$~nt ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$, $\ge 19$ SNPs), \RhizAeon climbs to $90.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall with $\hat{K} = 0.90\text{--}1.05$ inferred events, $98.1\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision, $0.00\%$ background false alarms in 14 of 15 conditions ($\le 0.50\%$ overall), and $14.5\text{--}27.7$~nt mean breakpoint error at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$ ($L_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 400$~nt, $J = 0.94\text{--}0.98$, Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}D).
Crucially, comparing raw \texttt{3SEQ} against \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} at $L_{\mathrm{tract}} \in \{400, 800\}$~nt ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$) confirms that the apparent explosion of alerts in unconsolidated 3SEQ ($\hat{K} = 15.8\text{--}40.0$ triplets and $46.0\%\text{--}99.8\%$ background taxa appearing in reciprocally inverted triplets) is entirely an artifact of raw triplet output: \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} reduces the inferred event count to $\hat{K} = 1.00\text{--}1.10$, suppresses background false alarms to $0.0\%\text{--}0.3\%$, and localizes breakpoints within $10.5\text{--}13.5$~nt ($J = 0.97\text{--}0.99$), while \RhizAeon achieves comparable consolidated event accuracy ($\hat{K} = 1.00\text{--}1.05$, $0.0\%\text{--}0.5\%$ background false alarms) and full co-recombinant cluster recovery ($100.0\%$ vs.\ $20.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall) in faster runtime.

\subsection{The Continuous Operational Phase Envelope of Recombination Deconvolution}
\label{subsec:grand_sweep_envelope}
In aeronautical engineering, an aircraft operating envelope defines certified boundaries of stable flight across airspeed, altitude, stall limits, and atmospheric turbulence.
Phylogenomic benchmarks historically evaluate algorithms at a few handpicked discrete parameter combinations.
Evaluating sparse parameter grids obscures how methods behave under continuous evolutionary variation.
To establish the operational envelope of \RhizAeon, we conducted a grand continuous parameter sweep across 1,000,000 independent simulation trials (Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_phase_diagram} and Table~\ref{tab:grand_sweep_1m_envelope}).
Simulations varied continuously across alignment length ($L \in [300, 30{,}000]$~nt), cohort depth ($N \in [4, 250]$~taxa), parental divergence ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} \in [0.005, 0.35]$~subs/site), and continuous Gamma rate heterogeneity ($\alpha \in [0.2, 5.0]$) alongside strict molecular clock controls ($\alpha \ge 50$, Table~\ref{tab:grand_sweep_1m_envelope}).
The benchmark cohort comprised 333,334 non-recombinant null alignments and 666,666 recombinant alignments spanning single breakpoint recombinants, recombination cassettes, and multi-donor / multi-event recombination mosaicism ($K \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$).

The sweep demonstrates that recombination detection is not governed by sequence length relative to the length of the recombinationally acquired tract alone.
The governing physical quantity is the count of informative mutations distinguishing donor from recipient within the transferred tract ($M_{\mathrm{tract}} \approx L_{\mathrm{tract}} \times d_{\mathrm{parent}}$, Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_phase_diagram}A,B).
Below three informative mutations ($M_{\mathrm{tract}} \le 2$), detection power is 0.7\% (599 detections out of 85,564 trials, below the background false-positive floor).
A tract bearing fewer than three distinguishing mutations is statistically indistinguishable from stochastic Poisson mutation clustering under any calibrated test.
Power lifts to 4.7\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [3, 5]$ (3,209 / 68,609), accelerates to 26.3\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [6, 10]$ (20,393 / 77,568), and reaches 50.5\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [11, 15]$ (26,558 / 52,607).
Across denser tracts, sensitivity climbs to 69.9\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [16, 25]$ (49,928 / 71,427), 84.2\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [26, 50]$ (81,402 / 96,694), 90.3\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [51, 100]$ (76,443 / 84,676), and plateaus at 93.4\% when $M_{\mathrm{tract}} > 100$ (120,984 / 129,521; reaching $96.05\%$ for $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 500$).
The operational stall boundary forms a clean hyperbola in parameter space: certified detection requires $L_{\mathrm{tract}} \times d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 6\text{--}10$~informative SNPs (Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_phase_diagram}A).

Across 333,334 non-recombinant null alignments, \RhizAeon maintained an overall alignment-level false positive rate of 1.3377\% (4,459 false positives), strictly below the nominal $\alpha = 5.0\%$ family-wise error rate ceiling (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_multievent}A).
Under a strict molecular clock ($\alpha \ge 50$), the alignment-level false positive rate was 1.2243\% (1,020 out of 83,311 trials).
Under continuous Gamma heterotachy ($\alpha \in [0.2, 5.0]$), the rate remained strictly calibrated at 1.3755\% (3,439 out of 250,023 trials).
At the individual taxon level, false alarms totaled 0.0336\% (7,633 falsely flagged sequences across 22,744,905 evaluated leaves, $99.97\%$ per-taxon specificity).
The unpolarized ancestral root token functions as a passive aerodynamic damper and absorbs private terminal branch mutation bursts into node zero without destabilizing trajectory tracking.

Spatial breakpoint resolution landed within sub-gene margins (Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_phase_diagram}C).
Among matched events, median absolute error was 15.0~nt for single events ($K=1$), 11.0~nt for dual events ($K=2$), 9.0~nt for triple events ($K=3$), and 8.0~nt for quadruple events ($K=4$), sharpening to 8.0~nt across all tracts carrying $>100$ informative SNPs ($7.0$~nt for $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 500$).
Across multi-event architectures ($K_{\mathrm{true}} \ge 1$), matched bipartite event precision reached $93.9\%$ for single events, $96.1\%$ for dual events, $96.9\%$ for triple events, and $97.5\%$ for quadruple events (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_multievent}B).
Multi-donor recombinant mosaics ($P_1 \leftarrow P_2 + P_3$, $N = 179{,}233$) were resolved with 61.9\% overall power ($96.2\%$ at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 50$) and 0.479 event $F_1$ score ($0.807$ at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 50$), matching single-donor hybrids with $J = 0.880$ boundary overlap at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 50$ without mutual dipole interference (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_multievent}D).
Single breakpoint terminal crossovers were detected with higher sensitivity (69.7\% power across $N = 239{,}767$ trials, matched median $J = 0.968$) than double breakpoint intercalary cassettes (49.7\% power across $N = 426{,}899$ trials, matched median $J = 0.940$, Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_multievent}C).
Across distributed cluster infrastructure, the benchmark completed all 1,000,000 whole-genome simulations and scans with a median scan time of 844.2~ms per alignment.
For genomic surveillance, defining this operational envelope provides actionable interpretability: outside the three-SNP stall limit, negative results are biologically informative in that the absence of detection constitutes statistical evidence against recombination rather than an algorithmic blind spot.
