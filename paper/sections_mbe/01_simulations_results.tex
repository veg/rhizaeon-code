\subsection{Coalescent ARG Benchmark and Calibration Against 3SEQ}
\label{subsec:posada_benchmark}

To establish a baseline against 3SEQ---evaluated here as a fast, widely used representative among exact triplet-scanning algorithms---we benchmarked \RhizAeon across the 3,200 coalescent simulation scenarios of \citet{posada2001evaluation} ($N = 10$ taxa, $L = 1{,}000$~nt, Figure~\ref{fig:posada_benchmark}, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_posada_summary}).
Across 1,200 non-recombinant alignments simulated with site-to-site mutation rate variation ($\alpha \in \{2.0, 0.5, 0.05\}$), both algorithms maintained strict false positive control well below the nominal 5\% ceiling (Figure~\ref{fig:posada_benchmark}C).
On these small 10-taxon recombination-free alignments, 3SEQ recorded a 0.17\% false positive rate (2 / 1,200 trials, 95\% Wilson score interval [0.05\%, 0.60\%]).
\RhizAeon yielded 0.58\% (7 / 1,200 trials, 95\% Wilson score interval [0.28\%, 1.20\%]), with 0.0\% false positives across all 600 trials at $\theta \in \{10, 50\}$ and across 8 of the 12 rate-variation conditions.
Occasional false alarms occurred primarily at high sequence divergence ($\theta = 200$, accounting for 6 of the 7 false positives across $\alpha \in \{2.0, 0.5, 0.05\}$) due to stochastic homoplasy across the 1,000~nt locus, instead of concentrating under extreme site-to-site rate variation.
Because the \citet{posada2001evaluation} protocol models Gamma rate variation strictly across sites rather than heterotachously along individual lineages, 3SEQ remains well-calibrated in this regime.
When lineage-specific rate accelerations are introduced (Section~\ref{subsec:grand_sweep_envelope}), private mutations on a single branch lack directional affinity toward candidate donors and route harmlessly into \RhizAeon's root sink.

Across the 1,600 recombinant alignments ($\rho \in \{1, 4, 16, 64\}$), detection power depended primarily on mutational availability (Figure~\ref{fig:posada_benchmark}A).
In low-diversity datasets ($\theta = 10$, fewer than 25 variable sites), sensitivity was strictly limited by mutational density: both \RhizAeon and 3SEQ detected 1.0\%--22.0\% of alignments across $\rho \in [1, 64]$, where frequent recombination events split sequences into short segments carrying few mutations.
At higher mutational densities ($\theta \ge 50$), 3SEQ's more permissive default triplet threshold provided a sensitivity advantage at intermediate recombination rates (e.g., 73.0\% vs.\ 55.0\% at $\theta = 50, \rho = 4$), while both methods converged to 88.0\%--100.0\% power at $\rho \ge 16$ ($\theta \in \{100, 200\}$).
Even on compact 10-taxon alignments where $\binom{10}{3} = 120$ triplets require minimal combinatorial work, \RhizAeon executed in a mean of 13.2--26.2~ms per alignment across $\theta \in [10, 200]$ (17.4~ms across all 1,200 rate-variation nulls), compared to 27.6--36.8~ms for 3SEQ.
As cohort sizes expand to $N = 32\text{--}55$ taxa in the surveillance benchmarks below, \RhizAeon's $\mathcal{O}(N K L)$ trajectory architecture runs up to 5.5-fold faster than $\mathcal{O}(N^3 L)$ triplet enumeration.

A central distinction between \RhizAeon and raw 3SEQ outputs lies in event cardinality and bipartite event precision (Figure~\ref{fig:posada_benchmark}B and Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_posada_summary}).
Because our Hudson coalescent simulator explicitly records the simulated Ancestral Recombination Graph (ARG) and extracts all observable topological transfers ($K_{\mathrm{true}}$, Section~\ref{supp:bipartite_matching_full}), we directly compared inferred event counts ($\hat{K}$) against both the exact simulated ARG events and Hudson's theoretical expectation ($\mathbb{E}[R]$, dashed line).
When 3SEQ is run without downstream post-processing, it outputs every sequence triplet displaying a significant hypergeometric random-walk excursion.
Even when an alignment contains only a single recombinant sequence, multiple combinations of parent-like proxy sequences in the alignment—compounded by co-recombinant descendants inheriting the same ancestral crossover—cause raw triplet counts to reach $\hat{K} = 16.3\text{--}17.3$ at $\theta = 200, \rho \in \{16, 64\}$.
In contrast, \RhizAeon analyzes all sequences jointly through iterative scrubbing and leave-one-in verification, grouping shared segments into consolidated ancestral events ($\hat{K} \approx 1.23\text{--}4.06$ at $\theta = 200, \rho \ge 4$).
Under strict one-to-one Hungarian bipartite matching weighted by parental triad attribution ($S_{\mathrm{triad}}$) and SNP overlap ($J_{\mathrm{SNP}}$, Equation~\ref{eq:methods_event_affinity}), \RhizAeon achieves 2.3- to 2.8-fold higher mean continuous event-level precision than unconsolidated 3SEQ across $\theta \ge 50$ ($36.5\%\text{--}63.2\%$ vs.\ $4.6\%\text{--}46.5\%$ at $\theta = 200$).
However, as emphasized in the 3SEQ documentation \citep{lam20183seq}, raw 3SEQ triplet lists are intended to be post-processed via downstream consolidation scripts that deduplicate redundant proxy-parent triplets and group overlapping breakpoint intervals.
We therefore implemented a faithful downstream 3SEQ consolidation pipeline---\texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)}---and evaluated it head-to-head against both raw \texttt{3SEQ} and \RhizAeon across four specialized epidemiological and evolutionary benchmark suites.

\subsection{Performance Across Specialized Surveillance and Evolutionary Regimes}
\label{subsec:specialized_scenarios}

Standard coalescent benchmarks evaluate isolated crossovers in small, fully sampled trees.
In natural populations and disease outbreaks, however, recombination violates these idealized conditions (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}).
Recombinant lineages expand into co-recombinant transmission clusters, acquire sequence tracts from multiple distinct donors, inherit material from unsampled reservoirs, or exchange short micro-conversion tracts.
To evaluate breakpoint localization, parent attribution, and cluster consolidation fairly, we compared \RhizAeon against both unconsolidated \texttt{3SEQ} and post-processed \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} across 2,280 alignments spanning four specialized regimes.

\paragraph{Epidemic Clonal Expansion}
During viral outbreaks and bacterial epidemics, an ancestral recombinant frequently transmits across multiple hosts and expands into a clade or cluster of $K$ sampled co-recombinant isolates (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}A).
Across 300 simulated outbreak cohorts ($N = 42\text{--}55$ taxa, $L = 5{,}000$~nt, $K \in \{2, 5, 8, 10, 15\}$), unconsolidated \texttt{3SEQ} reported every significant sequence triplet independently.
Raw triplet counts inflated from $\hat{K} = 9.5\text{--}30.5$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$) to $36.8\text{--}55.0$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$), while reciprocal triplet inversions flagged background parental lineages as recombinants.
Downstream consolidation in \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} filters reciprocal parent inversions via directional transition contrast and clusters overlapping co-recombinant intervals.
At moderate-to-high divergence ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$), this post-processing resolved the single-event epidemic regime (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}A).
It collapsed the co-recombinant cluster into $\hat{K} = 1.00\text{--}1.10$ unique events with $99.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall, $93.8\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision, $\le 2.5\%$ background false alarms, and $5.6\text{--}14.0$~nt mean breakpoint error ($J = 0.98\text{--}0.99$).
\RhizAeon achieved comparable consolidation and accuracy directly within its native linear-time pass ($\hat{K} = 1.00\text{--}1.25$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ and $\hat{K} = 1.00$ across all $K \in \{8, 10, 15\}$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \in \{0.02, 0.05, 0.10\}$).
It recovered $100.0\%$ of co-recombinant descendants across all 10 conditions at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ ($95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$, with $100.0\%$ in 14 of 15 conditions overall) at $92.3\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision ($100.0\%$ in 10 of 15 conditions) and $0.00\%$ background false positives in 11 of 15 conditions.
Mean breakpoint error was $12.4\text{--}39.3$~nt at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$ ($J = 0.95\text{--}0.98$) and $22.3\text{--}35.2$~nt at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.05$ ($J = 0.95\text{--}0.97$), with scans completing in $2.05\text{--}6.46$~s versus $1.06\text{--}10.87$~s for 3SEQ.
At low parental divergence ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$), \RhizAeon recovered a substantially higher fraction of co-recombinant cluster members than \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} as cluster size grew ($100.0\%$ recall vs.\ $28.0\%\text{--}45.0\%$ for $K \in \{8, 10, 15\}$).

\paragraph{Multi-Way Mosaic Clusters}
Natural viral and bacterial recombinants frequently acquire genetic material from more than one parental donor, forming multi-way mosaic architectures including dual cassettes, nested "Russian doll" transfers, and serial triple crossovers (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}B).
\RhizAeon accommodates multi-parent mosaicism by iteratively scrubbing detected segments and tracking directional excursions against all landmark references simultaneously.
A single query genome can thus be decomposed into multiple distinct donor transitions.
In Dual Cassette recombinants ($P_2$ and $P_3$ cassettes embedded within a $P_1$ background), \RhizAeon recovered both parental donors in nearly every trial, averaging $1.97$ of $2.00$ expected donors overall ($1.90\text{--}2.00$) and $2.00$ across all conditions at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}B).
Across all Dual Cassette conditions, recall reached $100.0\%$ with $97.1\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision ($100.0\%$ in 7 of 9 conditions), $\le 1.6\%$ background false alarms ($0.0\%$ across all $K$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$), and high boundary overlap ($J = 0.95\text{--}0.97$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$).
In Nested Russian Dolls ($P_1 \to P_2 \to P_3 \to P_2 \to P_1$) and Triple Crossovers ($P_1 \to P_2 \to P_3$), \RhizAeon resolved an average of $1.56$ ($1.40\text{--}1.75$) and $1.04$ ($0.70\text{--}1.35$) donors respectively while maintaining $95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall, $95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision, and $\le 1.7\%$ background false positives ($0.0\%$ in 13 of 18 conditions).
By contrast, 3SEQ's hypergeometric scan extracts a single dominant recombinant segment per triplet call.
Even after downstream consolidation, \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} recovered only one donor per mosaic genome ($1.00\text{--}1.15$ of $2.00$ expected donors in Dual Cassettes and $1.00$ in Nested Dolls, Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}B).
Furthermore, when a mosaic genome carries tracts from two distinct donors ($P_2$ and $P_3$), evaluating triplet $(R, P_1, P_2)$ across the $P_3$-derived segment distorts the flanking baseline.
Consequently, \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} recorded $16.2\%\text{--}32.9\%$ background false-positive calls in Dual Cassettes at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ (down from $47.1\%\text{--}81.0\%$ in raw 3SEQ, compared to $0.0\%\text{--}0.6\%$ for \RhizAeon).

\paragraph{Ghost Donor Introgression}
In empirical surveillance, recombination frequently involves donor lineages absent from the analyzed alignment, such as unsampled wildlife reservoirs or extinct ancestral variants (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}C).
In this regime, 3SEQ does not claim to identify the true unsampled parent.
Instead, it uses sampled sequences in the alignment as proxy parents to detect the localized shift in sequence similarity and localize breakpoint boundaries.
When a close relative of the unsampled donor is present in the cohort (the Surrogate Sister regime), both \RhizAeon and \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} perform well.
At $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$, \RhizAeon achieved $100.0\%$ recombinant recall, $100.0\%$ precision, $0.0\%$ background and parent false alarms, $J = 0.86\text{--}0.99$ boundary overlap ($0.96\text{--}0.99$ in 5 of 6 conditions), and $100.0\%$ surrogate sister attribution ($84.0\%\text{--}99.0\%$ recall, $90.6\%\text{--}96.3\%$ precision, and $85.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ attribution at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$).
Under the same conditions, \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} achieved $100.0\%$ recall, $88.7\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision, $J = 0.97\text{--}0.99$, and $95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ surrogate sister attribution, vastly improving upon unconsolidated 3SEQ ($9.3\%\text{--}39.3\%$ precision and $37.5\%\text{--}56.4\%$ background false alarms).
However, when the introgressing donor branches basal to the MRCA of the sampled alignment with no close sampled relative (the Pure Ghost and Deep Reservoir regimes), a critical trade-off emerges.
Unrooted 3SEQ triplets lack an outgroup reference to orient which sequence diverged.
An introgressed basal ghost tract in recombinant $R$ (within clade $P_1$) makes $R$ appear as a distant outgroup inside the tract, while non-recombinant $P_1$ sequences remain close to $P_1$ inside the tract and to proxy $P_2$ outside it.
Both raw \texttt{3SEQ} and \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} therefore localize the breakpoint coordinates accurately ($J = 0.95\text{--}0.99$) but invert the recombinant identity.
They fail to identify $R$ as the recombinant ($0.0\%$ recall for \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)}) and instead flag non-recombinant $P_1$ leaves in $50.0\%$ of background taxa ($100.0\%$ parent false alarm rate).
Instead of discarding basal introgressions or misattributing them to contemporary proxy clades, \RhizAeon's iterative scrubbing and orphan-divergence audit flags tracts where a sequence departs sharply from its home clade without matching any sampled reference, labeling the donor as \texttt{"Ghost"}.
Across Pure Ghost cohorts at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ ($K \in \{2, 5, 10\}$), \RhizAeon detects $85.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ of ghost-introgressed recombinants ($80.0\%\text{--}96.5\%$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$) with $95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision ($100.0\%$ in 5 of 6 conditions at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$), $J = 0.83\text{--}0.99$ boundary overlap, $85.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ \texttt{"Ghost"} donor attribution, and $0.0\%$ background false alarms in 5 of 6 conditions (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}C).
Similarly, across Deep Reservoir cohorts ($d_{\mathrm{reservoir}} = 0.15$), \RhizAeon achieves $80.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall ($90.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ in 8 of 9 conditions) and $80.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ \texttt{"Ghost"} donor attribution. At $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$, precision reaches $90.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ ($100.0\%$ in 5 of 6 conditions; $76.8\%\text{--}95.0\%$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$) with $J = 0.83\text{--}0.98$ boundary overlap and $0.0\%$ background false alarms in 5 of 6 conditions.

\paragraph{Bacterial Micro-Conversions}
In bacterial transformation (e.g., \emph{Streptococcus pneumoniae}, \emph{Helicobacter pylori}), homologous replacement frequently involves short micro-conversion tracts spanning $<10\%$ of the locus (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}D).
Detecting micro-conversions is governed by the information-theoretic count of distinguishing polymorphisms within the tract.
At $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 50$~nt ($L = 5{,}000$~nt), simulated tracts carried a mean of only $0.8\text{--}1.4$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$), $1.8\text{--}2.9$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.05$), and $4.9\text{--}5.2$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$) mutations.
Both \RhizAeon's windowless dyadic matched filter and 3SEQ's exact hypergeometric scan enforce strict family-wise error rate thresholds across all sequence comparisons.
When 50~nt tracts carried fewer than two mutations ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$), no method called recombination ($0.0\%$ recall and $0.0\%$ false positives).
As mutational density rose to $2.8\text{--}5.2$ SNPs at $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 50$~nt ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} \in \{0.05, 0.10\}$), \RhizAeon's multi-scale matched filter detected $15.0\%\text{--}30.0\%$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.05, K \in \{2, 5\}$) and $50.0\%\text{--}54.5\%$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$) of micro-conversions with $100.0\%$ precision and $0.0\%$ background false alarms, compared to $0.0\%\text{--}10.5\%$ for raw \texttt{3SEQ} and $0.0\%\text{--}5.0\%$ for \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)}.
At $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 100$~nt ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$, $8.0\text{--}10.6$ SNPs), \RhizAeon reached $80.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall (mean $88.3\%$) with $100.0\%$ precision and $0.0\%$ background false alarms, exceeding both raw \texttt{3SEQ} ($45.0\%\text{--}70.5\%$) and \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} ($20.0\%\text{--}45.0\%$).
Across all 45 Micro-Conversion conditions ($L_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [50, 800]$~nt), \RhizAeon maintained $100.0\%$ precision and $0.00\%$ background false alarms.
For tract lengths $L_{\mathrm{tract}} \in \{200, 400, 800\}$~nt ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$, $\ge 9.3$ SNPs), \RhizAeon achieved $85.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall ($100.0\%$ in 15 of 18 conditions) with $\hat{K} = 0.85\text{--}1.05$ inferred events ($1.00$ in 14 of 18 conditions).
Mean breakpoint error at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$ was $18.5\text{--}28.0$~nt at $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 200$~nt ($J = 0.88\text{--}0.91$), $15.6\text{--}28.1$~nt at $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 400$~nt ($J = 0.94\text{--}0.96$), and $14.5\text{--}22.2$~nt for $K \in \{2, 10\}$ at $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 800$~nt ($J = 0.93\text{--}0.98$, Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}D).
Comparing raw \texttt{3SEQ} against \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} at $L_{\mathrm{tract}} \in \{400, 800\}$~nt ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$) confirms that the apparent alert inflation in unconsolidated 3SEQ ($\hat{K} = 15.8\text{--}40.0$ triplets and $46.0\%\text{--}99.8\%$ background taxa in reciprocally inverted triplets) is an artifact of raw triplet output.
Post-processing in \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} reduces inferred events to $\hat{K} = 1.00\text{--}1.10$, suppresses background false alarms to $0.0\%\text{--}0.3\%$, and localizes breakpoints within $10.5\text{--}13.5$~nt ($J = 0.97\text{--}0.99$).
\RhizAeon achieves comparable consolidated event counts ($\hat{K} = 0.95\text{--}1.00$, $0.0\%$ false alarms) and higher co-recombinant cluster recovery ($95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ vs.\ $20.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall) in $0.37\text{--}2.03$~s per alignment.

\subsection{The Continuous Operational Phase Envelope of Recombination Deconvolution}
\label{subsec:grand_sweep_envelope}
In aeronautical engineering, an aircraft operating envelope defines certified boundaries of stable flight across airspeed, altitude, stall limits, and atmospheric turbulence.
Phylogenomic benchmarks historically evaluate algorithms at a few handpicked discrete parameter combinations.
Evaluating sparse parameter grids obscures how methods behave under continuous evolutionary variation.
To establish the operational envelope of \RhizAeon, we conducted a grand continuous parameter sweep across 1,000,000 independent simulation trials (Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_phase_diagram} and Table~\ref{tab:grand_sweep_1m_envelope}).
Simulations varied continuously across alignment length ($L \in [300, 30{,}000]$~nt), cohort depth ($N \in [4, 250]$~taxa), parental divergence ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} \in [0.005, 0.35]$~subs/site), and continuous Gamma rate heterogeneity ($\alpha \in [0.2, 5.0]$) alongside strict molecular clock controls ($\alpha \ge 50$, Table~\ref{tab:grand_sweep_1m_envelope}).
The benchmark cohort comprised 333,334 non-recombinant null alignments and 666,666 recombinant alignments spanning single breakpoint recombinants, recombination cassettes, and multi-donor / multi-event recombination mosaicism ($K \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$).

The sweep demonstrates that recombination detection is not governed by sequence length relative to the length of the recombinationally acquired tract alone.
The governing physical quantity is the count of informative mutations distinguishing donor from recipient within the transferred tract ($M_{\mathrm{tract}} \approx L_{\mathrm{tract}} \times d_{\mathrm{parent}}$, Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_phase_diagram}A,B).
Below three informative mutations ($M_{\mathrm{tract}} \le 2$), detection power is 0.6\% (484 detections out of 85,564 trials, below the background false-positive floor).
A tract bearing fewer than three distinguishing mutations is statistically indistinguishable from stochastic Poisson mutation clustering under any calibrated test.
Power lifts to 7.0\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [3, 5]$ (4,801 / 68,609), accelerates to 32.3\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [6, 10]$ (25,024 / 77,568), and reaches 56.4\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [11, 15]$ (29,679 / 52,607).
Across denser tracts, sensitivity climbs to 72.9\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [16, 25]$ (52,042 / 71,427), 83.3\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [26, 50]$ (80,562 / 96,694), 86.7\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [51, 100]$ (73,443 / 84,676), and plateaus at 88.4\% when $M_{\mathrm{tract}} > 100$ (114,518 / 129,521; reaching $91.83\%$ for $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 500$).
The operational stall boundary forms a clean hyperbola in parameter space: certified detection requires $L_{\mathrm{tract}} \times d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 6\text{--}10$~informative SNPs (Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_phase_diagram}A).

Across 333,334 non-recombinant null alignments, \RhizAeon maintained an overall alignment-level false positive rate of 1.0062\% (3,354 false positives), strictly below the nominal $\alpha = 5.0\%$ family-wise error rate ceiling (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_multievent}A).
Under a strict molecular clock ($\alpha \ge 50$), the alignment-level false positive rate was 0.9134\% (761 out of 83,311 trials).
Under continuous Gamma heterotachy ($\alpha \in [0.2, 5.0]$), the rate remained strictly calibrated at 1.0371\% (2,593 out of 250,023 trials).
At the individual taxon level, false alarms totaled 0.0259\% (5,884 falsely flagged sequences across 22,744,905 evaluated leaves, $99.974\%$ per-taxon specificity).
The unpolarized ancestral root token functions as a passive aerodynamic damper and absorbs private terminal branch mutation bursts into node zero without destabilizing trajectory tracking.

Spatial breakpoint resolution landed within sub-gene margins (Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_phase_diagram}C).
Among matched events, median absolute error was 14.0~nt for single events ($K=1$), 10.0~nt for dual events ($K=2$), 8.0~nt for triple events ($K=3$), and 8.0~nt for quadruple events ($K=4$), sharpening to 7.0~nt across all tracts carrying $>100$ informative SNPs ($8.0$~nt for $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 500$).
Across multi-event architectures ($K_{\mathrm{true}} \ge 1$), matched bipartite event precision reached $94.5\%$ for single events, $96.7\%$ for dual events, $97.5\%$ for triple events, and $97.9\%$ for quadruple events (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_multievent}B).
Multi-donor recombinant mosaics ($P_1 \leftarrow P_2 + P_3$, $N = 179{,}233$) were resolved with 61.6\% overall power ($91.7\%$ at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 50$) and 0.488 event $F_1$ score ($0.794$ at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 50$), matching single-donor hybrids with $J = 0.882$ boundary overlap at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 50$ without mutual dipole interference (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_multievent}D).
Single breakpoint terminal crossovers were detected with higher sensitivity (68.2\% power across $N = 239{,}767$ trials, matched median $J = 0.970$) than double breakpoint intercalary cassettes (50.9\% power across $N = 426{,}899$ trials, matched median $J = 0.944$, Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_multievent}C).
Across distributed cluster infrastructure, the benchmark completed all 1,000,000 whole-genome simulations and scans with a median scan time of 885.0~ms per alignment.
For genomic surveillance, defining this operational envelope provides actionable interpretability: outside the three-SNP stall limit, negative results are biologically informative in that the absence of detection constitutes statistical evidence against recombination rather than an algorithmic blind spot.
