\subsection{Coalescent ARG Benchmark and Calibration Against 3SEQ}
\label{subsec:posada_benchmark}

To benchmark \RhizAeon against 3SEQ \citep{boni2007exact,lam20183seq}—a fast, widely used exact triplet scanner—we evaluated both methods across the 3,200 coalescent simulation trials of \citet{posada2001evaluation} ($N = 10$ taxa, $L = 1{,}000$~nt, Figure~\ref{fig:posada_benchmark}, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_posada_summary}). Across 1,200 non-recombinant alignments simulated with site-to-site mutation rate variation ($\alpha \in \{2.0, 0.5, 0.05\}$), both methods held false-positive rates well below the nominal 5\% threshold (Figure~\ref{fig:posada_benchmark}C). On these 10-taxon null alignments, 3SEQ recorded a 0.17\% false-positive rate (2 / 1,200 trials, 95\% Wilson score interval [0.05\%, 0.60\%]), while \RhizAeon recorded 0.58\% (7 / 1,200 trials, 95\% Wilson score interval [0.28\%, 1.20\%]), with 0.0\% false positives across all 600 trials at $\theta \in \{10, 50\}$ and across 8 of the 12 rate-variation conditions. Occasional false positives occurred mainly at high sequence divergence ($\theta = 200$, accounting for 6 of the 7 false positives across $\alpha \in \{2.0, 0.5, 0.05\}$) due to random homoplasy across the 1,000~nt locus rather than extreme rate variation. Because the \citet{posada2001evaluation} protocol varies mutation rates across sites rather than along individual branches, 3SEQ remains well-calibrated here. When lineage-specific rate accelerations occur (Section~\ref{subsec:grand_sweep_envelope}), private mutations on a single branch lack directional affinity toward contemporary donors and route harmlessly into \RhizAeon's root sink.

Across the 1,600 recombinant alignments ($\rho \in \{1, 4, 16, 64\}$), detection power depended primarily on mutation density (Figure~\ref{fig:posada_benchmark}A). In low-diversity datasets ($\theta = 10$, fewer than 25 variable sites), sparse mutations limited sensitivity: both \RhizAeon and 3SEQ detected 1.0\%--22.0\% of alignments across $\rho \in [1, 64]$, where frequent crossovers split sequences into short segments carrying few mutations. At higher mutation densities ($\theta \ge 50$), 3SEQ's more permissive default triplet threshold gave it higher sensitivity at intermediate recombination rates (e.g., 73.0\% vs.\ 55.0\% at $\theta = 50, \rho = 4$), while both methods reached 88.0\%--100.0\% power at $\rho \ge 16$ ($\theta \in \{100, 200\}$). Even on small 10-taxon alignments with only $\binom{10}{3} = 120$ triplets, \RhizAeon ran in a mean of 13.2--26.2~ms per alignment across $\theta \in [10, 200]$ (17.4~ms across all 1,200 rate-variation nulls), compared to 27.6--36.8~ms for 3SEQ. As cohort sizes grow to $N = 100\text{--}1{,}500$ taxa in the outbreak scaling benchmark below, \RhizAeon's $\mathcal{O}(N K L)$ architecture runs 73.8- to 224.0-fold faster than 3SEQ's $\mathcal{O}(N^3 L)$ triplet scan at $N = 100\text{--}150$ and finishes $N = 1{,}500$ in 28.1~s.

A key difference between \RhizAeon and raw 3SEQ outputs is event count ($\hat{K}$) and bipartite event precision (Figure~\ref{fig:posada_benchmark}B and Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp_posada_summary}). Because our Hudson coalescent simulator records the full Ancestral Recombination Graph (ARG) and extracts all observable topological transfers ($K_{\mathrm{true}}$, Supplementary Section~\ref{supp:bipartite_matching_full}), we compared inferred event counts ($\hat{K}$) against both the true simulated ARG events and Hudson's theoretical expectation ($\mathbb{E}[R]$, dashed line). Without post-processing, 3SEQ outputs every sequence triplet with a significant hypergeometric excursion. Even when an alignment contains only one recombinant sequence, multiple combinations of proxy parent sequences—compounded by co-recombinant descendants sharing the same ancestral crossover—inflate raw triplet counts to $\hat{K} = 16.3\text{--}17.3$ at $\theta = 200, \rho \in \{16, 64\}$. In contrast, \RhizAeon analyzes all sequences jointly via iterative scrubbing and leave-one-in verification, grouping shared segments into consolidated ancestral events ($\hat{K} \approx 1.23\text{--}4.06$ at $\theta = 200, \rho \ge 4$). Under one-to-one Hungarian bipartite matching weighted by parental triad attribution ($S_{\mathrm{triad}}$) and SNP overlap ($J_{\mathrm{SNP}}$, Materials and Methods, Equation~\ref{eq:methods_event_affinity}), \RhizAeon achieves 2.3- to 2.8-fold higher mean event-level precision than unconsolidated 3SEQ across $\theta \ge 50$ ($36.5\%\text{--}63.2\%$ vs.\ $4.6\%\text{--}46.5\%$ at $\theta = 200$). However, as noted in the 3SEQ documentation \citep{boni2007exact,lam20183seq}, raw 3SEQ triplet lists are designed to be post-processed to remove redundant proxy-parent triplets and merge overlapping breakpoint intervals. We therefore implemented a downstream 3SEQ consolidation pipeline—\texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)}—and compared it directly against both raw \texttt{3SEQ} and \RhizAeon across four specialized benchmark suites.

\subsection{Performance Across Specialized Surveillance and Evolutionary Regimes}
\label{subsec:specialized_scenarios}

Standard coalescent benchmarks test isolated crossovers in small, fully sampled trees, whereas real-world datasets rarely match these ideals (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}). In natural populations, recombinant lineages expand into co-recombinant transmission clusters and acquire tracts ranging from $<1\%$ to $50\%$ of the genome from multiple or unsampled donors. To evaluate breakpoint localization, parent attribution, and cluster consolidation fairly, we compared \RhizAeon against both unconsolidated \texttt{3SEQ} and post-processed \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} across 2,280 alignments spanning four specialized regimes (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}, Cols.~1--4).

\paragraph{Epidemic Clonal Expansion}
During viral outbreaks and bacterial epidemics, an ancestral recombinant often spreads across hosts to form a cluster of $K_{\mathrm{desc}}$ sampled co-recombinant isolates (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}A). Across 300 simulated outbreak cohorts ($N = 42\text{--}55$ taxa, $L = 5{,}000$~nt, $K_{\mathrm{desc}} \in \{2, 5, 8, 10, 15\}$), unconsolidated \texttt{3SEQ} reported every significant triplet separately, inflating raw triplet counts from $\hat{K} = 9.5\text{--}30.5$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$) to $36.8\text{--}55.0$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$) and flagging background parents via reciprocal triplet inversions. Downstream post-processing in \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} filters out reciprocal parent inversions using directional transition contrast and merges overlapping co-recombinant intervals. At moderate-to-high divergence ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$), this consolidation resolved the single ancestral event (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}A, Cols.~2--4), collapsing the cluster into $\hat{K} = 1.00\text{--}1.10$ events with $99.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall, $93.8\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision, $\le 2.5\%$ background false positives, and $5.6\text{--}14.0$~nt mean breakpoint error ($J = 0.98\text{--}0.99$). \RhizAeon achieved comparable consolidation and accuracy directly in its linear-time pass ($\hat{K} = 1.00\text{--}1.25$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$, and $\hat{K} = 1.00$ across 8 of 9 conditions for $K_{\mathrm{desc}} \in \{8, 10, 15\}$ with $\hat{K} = 1.10$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02, K_{\mathrm{desc}} = 15$; Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}A, Col.~4). It recovered $100.0\%$ of co-recombinant descendants across all 10 conditions at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ ($95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$, and $100.0\%$ in 14 of 15 conditions overall) at $92.3\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision ($100.0\%$ in 10 of 15 conditions) and $0.00\%$ background false positives in 11 of 15 conditions. Mean breakpoint error was $12.4\text{--}39.3$~nt at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$ ($J = 0.95\text{--}0.98$) and $22.3\text{--}35.2$~nt at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.05$ ($J = 0.95\text{--}0.97$; Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}A, Col.~3), completing scans in $2.05\text{--}6.46$~s versus $1.13\text{--}4.01$~s for 3SEQ. At low parental divergence ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$), \RhizAeon recovered a much higher fraction of co-recombinant cluster members than \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} as cluster size grew ($100.0\%$ recall vs.\ $28.0\%\text{--}45.0\%$ for $K_{\mathrm{desc}} \in \{8, 10, 15\}$; dashed curves in Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}A, Col.~2).

\paragraph{Multi-Way Mosaic Clusters}
Viral and bacterial recombinants often acquire segments from multiple donors, forming dual cassettes, nested ``Russian doll'' transfers, or serial triple crossovers (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}B, Col.~1). By iteratively scrubbing detected segments and tracking excursions against all landmarks simultaneously, \RhizAeon decomposes a single query genome into multiple donor transitions. In Dual Cassette recombinants ($P_2$ and $P_3$ cassettes in a $P_1$ background), \RhizAeon recovered both donors in nearly every trial, averaging $1.97$ of $2.00$ expected donors overall ($1.90\text{--}2.00$) and $2.00$ across all conditions at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}B, Col.~2). Across all Dual Cassette conditions, recall reached $100.0\%$ with $97.1\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision ($100.0\%$ in 7 of 9 conditions), $\le 1.6\%$ background false positives ($0.0\%$ across all $K_{\mathrm{desc}}$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$), and high boundary overlap ($J = 0.97\text{--}0.98$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$, and $J = 0.95\text{--}0.98$ across $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$; Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}B, Col.~3). In Nested Russian Dolls ($P_1 \to P_2 \to P_3 \to P_2 \to P_1$) and Triple Crossovers ($P_1 \to P_2 \to P_3$), \RhizAeon resolved an average of $1.56$ ($1.40\text{--}1.75$) and $1.04$ ($0.70\text{--}1.35$) donors respectively while maintaining $95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall, $95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision, and $\le 1.7\%$ background false positives ($0.0\%$ in 13 of 18 conditions). By contrast, 3SEQ's hypergeometric scan extracts only one dominant segment per triplet call, so even \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} recovered only one donor per mosaic genome ($1.00\text{--}1.15$ of $2.00$ donors in Dual Cassettes and $1.00$ in Nested Dolls, Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}B, Col.~2). Moreover, when a mosaic carries tracts from two donors ($P_2$ and $P_3$), evaluating triplet $(R, P_1, P_2)$ across the $P_3$ segment distorts the flanking baseline, causing \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} to record $16.2\%\text{--}32.9\%$ background false positives in Dual Cassettes at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ (down from $47.1\%\text{--}81.0\%$ in raw 3SEQ, vs.\ $0.0\%\text{--}0.11\%$ for \RhizAeon at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ and $0.0\%\text{--}1.6\%$ overall).

\paragraph{Ghost Donor Introgression}
In empirical surveillance, donor lineages are frequently absent from the alignment, such as unsampled wildlife reservoirs or extinct variants (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}C, Col.~1). In this regime, 3SEQ uses sampled sequences as proxy parents to detect similarity shifts and localize breakpoints. When a close relative of the unsampled donor is present (the Surrogate Sister regime), both \RhizAeon and \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} perform well. At $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$, \RhizAeon achieved $100.0\%$ recall, $100.0\%$ precision, $0.0\%$ background and parent false positives, $J = 0.86\text{--}0.99$ boundary overlap ($0.96\text{--}0.99$ in 5 of 6 conditions), and $100.0\%$ surrogate sister attribution ($84.0\%\text{--}99.0\%$ recall, $90.6\%\text{--}96.3\%$ precision, and $85.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ attribution at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$; Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}C, Cols.~2--3). Under the same conditions, \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} achieved $100.0\%$ recall, $88.7\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision, $J = 0.97\text{--}0.99$, and $95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ surrogate sister attribution, improving substantially over raw 3SEQ ($9.3\%\text{--}39.3\%$ precision and $37.5\%\text{--}56.4\%$ background false positives; Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}C, Cols.~2--3). However, when the donor branches basal to the alignment MRCA with no close sampled relative (the Pure Ghost and Deep Reservoir regimes), unrooted 3SEQ triplets lack an outgroup to orient which sequence diverged. A basal ghost tract in recombinant $R$ (within clade $P_1$) makes $R$ look like a distant outgroup inside the tract, while non-recombinant $P_1$ sequences stay close to $P_1$ inside the tract and to proxy $P_2$ outside it. Both raw \texttt{3SEQ} and \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} therefore localize the breakpoints accurately ($J = 0.95\text{--}0.99$) but invert the recombinant identity: they miss $R$ ($0.0\%$ recall for \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)}) and falsely flag non-recombinant $P_1$ leaves in $50.0\%$ of background taxa ($100.0\%$ parent false-positive rate; Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}C, Col.~2). In contrast, \RhizAeon's iterative scrubbing and orphan-divergence audit flags tracts where a sequence departs sharply from its home clade without matching any sampled reference, labeling the donor as \texttt{"Ghost"}. Across Pure Ghost cohorts at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$ ($K_{\mathrm{desc}} \in \{2, 5, 10\}$), \RhizAeon detects $85.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ of ghost-introgressed recombinants ($80.0\%\text{--}96.5\%$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$) with $95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ precision ($100.0\%$ in 5 of 6 conditions at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$), $J = 0.83\text{--}0.99$ boundary overlap, $85.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ \texttt{"Ghost"} donor attribution, and $0.0\%$ background false positives in 5 of 6 conditions (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}C). Across Deep Reservoir cohorts ($d_{\mathrm{reservoir}} = 0.15$), \RhizAeon similarly achieves $80.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall ($90.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ in 8 of 9 conditions) and $80.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ \texttt{"Ghost"} attribution; at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$, precision reaches $90.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ ($100.0\%$ in 5 of 6 conditions; $76.8\%\text{--}95.0\%$ at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$) with $J = 0.83\text{--}0.98$ boundary overlap and $0.0\%$ background false positives in 5 of 6 conditions.

\paragraph{Bacterial Micro-Conversions and Large-Alignment Scaling}
In bacterial transformation (e.g., \emph{Streptococcus pneumoniae}, \emph{Helicobacter pylori}), homologous replacement often transfers short ``micro-conversion'' tracts spanning $<10\%$ of a locus (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}D). Detecting micro-conversions depends on the number of distinguishing polymorphisms inside the tract. At $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 50$~nt ($L = 5{,}000$~nt), simulated tracts carried a mean of only $0.8\text{--}1.4$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$), $1.8\text{--}2.9$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.05$), and $4.9\text{--}5.2$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$) mutations. Because both \RhizAeon's matched filter and 3SEQ's hypergeometric scan enforce strict family-wise error control, neither method called recombination when 50~nt tracts carried fewer than two mutations ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.02$; $0.0\%$ recall and $0.0\%$ false positives). As mutation counts rose to $2.8\text{--}5.2$ SNPs at $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 50$~nt ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} \in \{0.05, 0.10\}$), \RhizAeon detected $15.0\%\text{--}30.0\%$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.05, K_{\mathrm{desc}} \in \{2, 5\}$) and $50.0\%\text{--}54.5\%$ ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$) of micro-conversions with $100.0\%$ precision and $0.0\%$ background false positives, compared to $0.0\%\text{--}10.5\%$ for raw \texttt{3SEQ} and $0.0\%\text{--}5.0\%$ for \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} (Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}D, Col.~2). At $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 100$~nt ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$, $8.0\text{--}10.6$ SNPs), \RhizAeon reached $80.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall (mean $88.3\%$) with $100.0\%$ precision and $0.0\%$ false positives, outperforming raw \texttt{3SEQ} ($45.0\%\text{--}70.5\%$) and \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} ($20.0\%\text{--}45.0\%$). Across all 45 Micro-Conversion conditions ($L_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [50, 800]$~nt), \RhizAeon maintained $100.0\%$ precision and $0.00\%$ background false positives. For $L_{\mathrm{tract}} \in \{200, 400, 800\}$~nt ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 0.05$, $\ge 9.3$ SNPs), \RhizAeon achieved $85.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ recall ($100.0\%$ in 15 of 18 conditions) with $\hat{K} = 0.85\text{--}1.05$ inferred events ($1.00$ in 14 of 18 conditions); mean summed two-boundary breakpoint error ($|\hat{u}_1 - u_1| + |\hat{u}_2 - u_2|$, or $7.3\text{--}14.1$~nt per boundary) at $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$ was $18.5\text{--}28.0$~nt at $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 200$~nt ($J = 0.88\text{--}0.91$), $15.6\text{--}28.1$~nt at $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 400$~nt ($J = 0.94\text{--}0.96$), and $14.5\text{--}22.2$~nt for $K_{\mathrm{desc}} \in \{2, 10\}$ at $L_{\mathrm{tract}} = 800$~nt ($J = 0.93\text{--}0.98$, Figure~\ref{fig:specialized_scenarios}D, Col.~3). At $L_{\mathrm{tract}} \in \{400, 800\}$~nt ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.10$), post-processing in \texttt{3SEQ (Consolidated)} reduced raw 3SEQ triplet counts from $\hat{K} = 15.8\text{--}40.0$ ($46.0\%\text{--}99.8\%$ background false positives) down to $\hat{K} = 1.00\text{--}1.10$ ($0.0\%\text{--}0.3\%$ false positives, $10.5\text{--}13.5$~nt error, $J = 0.97\text{--}0.99$), while \RhizAeon achieved $\hat{K} = 0.95\text{--}1.00$, $0.0\%$ false positives, and higher cluster recall ($95.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ vs.\ $20.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$) in $0.37\text{--}2.03$~s per alignment. Finally, across outbreak alignments scaling from $N = 50$ to $N = 1{,}500$ sequences ($L = 5{,}000$~nt, $d_{\mathrm{parent}} = 0.05$, $K_{\mathrm{desc}} \in \{10\text{--}150\}$), 3SEQ's runtime grew cubically from $2.96$~s ($N = 50$) to $37.93$~s ($N = 100$) and $160.62$~s ($N = 150$) before exceeding the $240$~s cutoff at $N \ge 250$ ($\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ extrapolated runtimes of $\sim 12.4$~min at $N = 250$, $\sim 1.65$~h at $N = 500$, $\sim 13.2$~h at $N = 1{,}000$, and $\sim 44.6$~h at $N = 1{,}500$). In contrast, \RhizAeon finished $N = 100$ in $0.51$~s ($73.8$-fold speedup), $N = 150$ in $0.72$~s ($224.0$-fold speedup), $N = 250$ in $1.26$~s, $N = 500$ in $3.24$~s, $N = 1{,}000$ in $11.73$~s, and $N = 1{,}500$ in $28.14$~s, recovering $100.0\%$ of recombinants at $100.0\%$ precision in a single event ($\hat{K} = 1.0$) at every cohort size.

\subsection{The Continuous Operational Phase Envelope of Recombination Deconvolution}
\label{subsec:grand_sweep_envelope}

Just as an aircraft operating envelope defines safe flight boundaries across speed, altitude, and turbulence, an algorithmic operating envelope maps detection limits across continuous evolutionary space rather than a few discrete grid points. We mapped \RhizAeon's operational envelope across 1,000,000 independent simulation trials (Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_phase_diagram} and Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:grand_sweep_1m_envelope}), varying alignment length ($L \in [1{,}000, 30{,}000]$~nt), cohort size ($N \in [4, 250]$~taxa), parental divergence ($d_{\mathrm{parent}} \in [0.005, 0.25]$~subs/site), and continuous Gamma rate heterogeneity ($\alpha \in [0.05, 5.0]$) alongside strict clock controls ($\alpha \ge 50$), spanning 333,334 clonal null alignments ($K_{\mathrm{true}} = 0$) and 666,666 recombinant alignments with single crossovers, cassettes, and multi-donor mosaics ($K_{\mathrm{true}} \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$). The sweep shows that recombination detection is governed not by relative tract length alone, but by the number of informative mutations separating donor and recipient inside the transferred tract ($M_{\mathrm{tract}} \approx L_{\mathrm{tract}} \times d_{\mathrm{parent}}$, Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_phase_diagram}A,B). With two or fewer informative mutations ($M_{\mathrm{tract}} \le 2$), power is 0.6\% (484 / 85,564 trials, below the null false-positive floor) because fewer than three mutations cannot be distinguished from random Poisson clustering. Power rises to 7.0\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [3, 5]$ (4,801 / 68,609), 32.3\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [6, 10]$ (25,024 / 77,568), 56.4\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [11, 15]$ (29,679 / 52,607), 72.9\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [16, 25]$ (52,042 / 71,427), 83.3\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [26, 50]$ (80,562 / 96,694), and 86.7\% at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \in [51, 100]$ (73,443 / 84,676), plateauing at 88.4\% when $M_{\mathrm{tract}} > 100$ (114,518 / 129,521; $91.83\%$ for $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 500$). The operational stall boundary forms a hyperbola in parameter space requiring $L_{\mathrm{tract}} \times d_{\mathrm{parent}} \ge 6\text{--}10$~informative SNPs (Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_phase_diagram}A).

Across all 333,334 non-recombinant null alignments, \RhizAeon held the alignment-level false-positive rate to 1.0062\% (3,354 false positives), well below the nominal $\alpha = 5.0\%$ ceiling (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_multievent}A): 0.9134\% (761 / 83,311) under a strict clock ($\alpha \ge 50$) and 1.0371\% (2,593 / 250,023) under continuous Gamma heterotachy ($\alpha \in [0.05, 5.0]$), corresponding to a per-taxon false-positive rate of 0.0259\% (5,884 / 22,744,905 leaves, $99.974\%$ specificity) as private branch mutations routed into the root sink. Among matched events, median breakpoint error was 14.0~nt for single events ($K_{\mathrm{true}}=1$), 10.0~nt for double events ($K_{\mathrm{true}}=2$), and 8.0~nt for triple and quadruple events ($K_{\mathrm{true}} \in \{3, 4\}$), sharpening to 7.0~nt for tracts with $>100$ informative SNPs ($8.0$~nt for $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 500$; Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_phase_diagram}C), while matched bipartite precision reached $94.5\%$, $96.7\%$, $97.5\%$, and $97.9\%$ for $K_{\mathrm{true}} = 1, 2, 3,$ and $4$ (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_multievent}B). Multi-donor mosaics ($P_1 \leftarrow P_2 + P_3$, $N = 179{,}233$) were resolved with 61.6\% overall power ($91.7\%$ at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 50$) and 0.488 event $F_1$ ($0.794$ at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 50$), matching single-donor boundary overlap ($J = 0.882$ at $M_{\mathrm{tract}} \ge 50$; Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_multievent}D), and single-breakpoint crossovers showed higher sensitivity (68.2\% across $N = 239{,}767$, median $J = 0.970$) than two-breakpoint cassettes (50.9\% across $N = 426{,}899$, median $J = 0.944$; Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:grand_sweep_1m_multievent}C). Across cluster nodes, all 1,000,000 trials completed with a median scan time of 885.0~ms per alignment. In practice, this envelope makes negative calls interpretable: above 15--25 informative SNPs (where sensitivity exceeds 73\%--88%), finding no recombination provides statistical evidence against reticulation rather than an algorithmic blind spot.
