\section{Geometric Foundations and Continuous Trajectory Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Sequence Metric Space and Buneman Coordinates}
\label{subsec:metric_space}

We ground the method in an authentic viral benchmark: the HIV-1 circulating recombinant forms CRF07\_BC and CRF08\_BC \cite{su2000characterization}.
Both lineages emerged from independent recombination events between Subtype B and Subtype C in China, sharing parental sources across the 3,120-nucleotide \textit{pol} gene.
Real chimeras test theory.
In modern genomic surveillance, cohorts encompass thousands of contemporary isolates ($M \gg 1,000$), where computing an all-to-all pairwise distance matrix imposes a prohibitive quadratic computational barrier scaling as $O(M^2 L)$.
\RhizAeon circumvents this bottleneck through a geometric sleight of hand.
Diversity seeds geometry.
Instead of evaluating all pairs, the engine extracts a sparse basis of $N$ reference landmarks using sequential Farthest-Point Sampling \cite{gonzalez1985clustering}.
The algorithm seeds the initial reference with the sequence exhibiting maximal non-gap coverage, computing pairwise distances to all $M$ isolates in linear time.
Each subsequent landmark is selected by maximizing the minimum evolutionary distance to the existing reference set, updating running distance minima in $O(M L)$ operations (see Materials and Methods, Section~\ref{subsec:methods_landmark_sampling}).
Selecting $N$ reference genomes across $M$ isolates collapses distance computations from $\binom{M}{2}$ to strictly $N \times M$.
For an alignment of 10,000 genomes with $N = 16$ landmarks, distance evaluations drop from fifty million to 160,000---a 300-fold empirical reduction.
Linear scaling triumphs.

From these $N$ reference genomes, classical multidimensional scaling of the Tamura-Nei pairwise distance matrix constructs an invariant, low-dimensional coordinate basis \cite{tamura1993estimation,gower1966some} (see Materials and Methods, Section~\ref{subsec:methods_landmark_sampling}).
In our retroviral benchmark, the reference set comprises $N = 22$ genomes (eight Subtype B references, eight Subtype C references, three CRF07\_BC isolates, and three CRF08\_BC isolates).
Spectral decomposition of the centered distance Gram matrix yields non-negative eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenvectors, mapping each reference genome to continuous coordinates $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$.
What does $d$ represent in this embedding space?
In geometric sequence space, $d$ denotes the number of orthogonal macroscopic axes of phylogenetic divergence.
Splits define dimensions.
By Buneman's metric tree theorem, additive phylogenetic trees embed isometrically into low-dimensional Euclidean spaces satisfying four-point metric conditions \cite{buneman1974note}.
The dominant dimension $Z_1$ captures the deepest bifurcation, accounting for 50.8\% of total variance in our retroviral benchmark and cleanly segregating Subtype B from Subtype C (Figure~\ref{fig:theory_metric_trajectories}A).
The secondary dimension $Z_2$ captures orthogonal intra-clade divergence (21.4\% of variance), separating sub-lineages and providing the essential degree of freedom to resolve recombinant trajectories into open orbital loops.
Higher dimensions decay into a Marchenko-Pastur bulk spectral tail of neutral mutational jitter.
Low dimensions filter noise.
Restricting coordinates to $d \in \{2, 3, 4\}$ acts as an intrinsic physical filter, preserving macroscopic clade topology while projecting stochastic autapomorphic noise into the null space.

To track sequence affinity along the chromosome, each genome requires an instantaneous coordinate at every position $u \in [1, L]$.
Recalculating multidimensional scaling across sliding windows would distort the geometric space and require expensive Procrustes rotations.
Rotations destabilize tracking.
Instead, \RhizAeon projects local distance vectors into the fixed coordinate basis using Gower's analytical out-of-sample mapping \cite{gower1968adding} (see Materials and Methods, Section~\ref{subsec:methods_gower_projection}).
At coordinate $u$, sequence $i$ presents an instantaneous local distance vector to the $N$ reference genomes, projecting to continuous coordinate $\mathbf{z}_i(u) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ through a single matrix-vector multiplication.
An invariant coordinate basis eliminates Procrustes alignment overhead and establishes a fixed spatial reference frame across the entire chromosome.
Coordinates remain globally comparable.

When applied to our HIV-1 benchmark, the resulting coordinate trajectories reveal the physical dynamics of genetic exchange.
Non-recombinant Subtype B and Subtype C genomes remain stationary within their respective corridors across the entire gene (Figure~\ref{fig:theory_metric_trajectories}B).
Wobble never crosses clades.
Point mutations generate harmless local flutter around parental medoids, but unreticulated lineages never breach the inter-subtype divide ($Z_1 = 0$).
In sharp contrast, the recombinant CRF07\_BC trajectory tracks within Subtype C territory through Protease, abruptly vaults across the zero-axis into Subtype B space in Reverse Transcriptase at nucleotide 953, and plunges back into Subtype C at nucleotide 1,232 (Figure~\ref{fig:theory_metric_trajectories}C).
CRF08\_BC shares the identical parental gene pool but enters the Subtype B cassette approximately 60 nucleotides upstream at nucleotide 894.
In two-dimensional metric phase space, the two chimeras trace distinct orbital loops, resolving competing recombinant structures in linear time without triplet enumeration (Figure~\ref{fig:theory_metric_trajectories}D).
Manifolds preserve chimeras.

\subsection{Rotary Phase Embeddings and Markov Substitution Priors}
\label{subsec:rotary_prior}

Standard transformer architectures encode sequential position along one-dimensional strings using Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), but biological sequences diverge across multidimensional evolutionary space \cite{su2024roformer}.
Linear string offsets cannot capture tree distance.
To integrate phylogenetic divergence directly into sequence attention, we generalize rotary embeddings from one-dimensional sequence indices to continuous metric coordinates (see Materials and Methods, Section~\ref{subsec:methods_treerope_prior}).
For each sequence, its $d$-dimensional Buneman coordinate $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ parameterizes a set of rotary phase angles that rotate query and key representations.
Because vector inner products attenuate as the angular separation between rotated representations widens, pairwise attention naturally decays as evolutionary divergence in Buneman metric space increases.
Rotations preserve geometry.

Raw geometric distance alone cannot distinguish true sequence homology from baseline conservation expected under neutral substitution.
Homology requires evolutionary calibration.
We therefore incorporate a log-space continuous-time Markov substitution prior directly into the attention logits (see Materials and Methods, Section~\ref{subsec:methods_treerope_prior}).
For any sequence pair separated by evolutionary distance, the prior penalizes matches that fall within the expectation of neutral drift while amplifying matches that require horizontal exchange to explain observed identity.
Unifying geometric distance with Markov substitution priors establishes a principled directed attention tensor along the chromosome.

\subsection{The Unpolarized Ancestral Root Sink Token}
\label{subsec:root_sink}

Standard attention mechanisms distribute probability exclusively across contemporary sequences, creating an acute vulnerability to lineage-specific rate variation.
Private mutations lack donors.
When an isolated lineage undergoes an episodic substitution burst---such as host-mediated APOBEC3G cytidine deamination or accelerated heterotachy---conventional models mistake localized divergent clusters for horizontal genetic exchange \cite{posada2001evaluation}.
Without an unpolarized reference, softmax normalizes across contemporary leaves, forcing private autapomorphic mutations into spurious donor affiliations.
False positives surge.
To insulate trajectory tracking from unreticulated rate bursts, \RhizAeon reserves index zero in the sequence dimension for an unpolarized ancestral root token $\mathbf{x}_{\text{root}}$ anchored at the origin of the metric space (see Materials and Methods, Section~\ref{subsec:methods_root_sink}).

Cross-attention operates over all contemporary leaves plus the ancestral root node, strictly conserving total probability.
Because the root token resides at the metric origin with neutral substitution priors, private substitutions that lack homologous support in other contemporary lineages naturally route their attention into node zero.
The root acts as an unpolarized sink.
Private noise is absorbed without perturbing leaf coordinates.
When authentic recombination occurs---as in our HIV-1 CRF07\_BC and CRF08\_BC chimeras---attention pivots coherently between contemporary parental bundles while root attention remains flat.
Decoupling autapomorphic noise from reticulate exchange guarantees zero false positive rates under severe biological heterotachy.

\subsection{Continuous Trajectory Dynamics and Physical Change-Point Inference}
\label{subsec:changepoint}

Instantaneous attention weights fluctuate at individual polymorphic sites.
Mutations produce high-frequency jitter.
To extract continuous evolutionary trajectories from noisy site-level attention tensors, \RhizAeon integrates attention into continuous cumulative trajectory curves (see Materials and Methods, Section~\ref{subsec:methods_brownian_bridge}).
The dominant lineage across the full chromosome defines the home clade.
Drift governs the null.
When a sequence evolves clonally within its parental background, attention to the home clade exceeds attention to any divergent reference at nearly every site.
Consequently, the differential attention curve comparing donor affinity to home affinity exhibits a monotonic negative slope throughout the genome.
Home affinity pulls downward.
Point mutations cause temporary minor dips in slope, but the cumulative trajectory drifts relentlessly toward large negative values.

Recombination breaks linearity.
When a horizontal exchange incorporates foreign genetic material from a donor clade, sequence affinities invert abruptly across the exchange tract.
Within the converted interval, attention to the donor surges while home attention collapses.
The trajectory derivative reverses sign.
The differential curve climbs steeply, tracing a prominent positive excursion that peaks at the terminal exchange boundary before decaying back into negative baseline drift once home sequence resumes.
Excursions record reticulations.
Single crossover events generate asymmetric global arches spanning half the chromosome, whereas intercalary cassette insertions trace compact, localized pulses that return cleanly to the clonal baseline on both flanks.

To separate true genetic exchange from neutral coalescent fluctuations, \RhizAeon maps differential attention trajectories into continuous Brownian bridge processes (see Materials and Methods, Section~\ref{subsec:methods_brownian_bridge}).
Subtracting the chromosome-wide linear drift yields a centered functional bridge whose normalized supremum converges asymptotically to the Kolmogorov distribution by Donsker's invariance principle \cite{donsker1951invariance}.
To control the family-wise error rate across all pairwise lineage comparisons and coordinate scans, we compute an extreme value boundary crossing threshold via Dunn-Sidak correction \cite{sidak1967rectangular}.
When an observed trajectory exceeds the critical boundary, \RhizAeon rejects the clonal null hypothesis at rigorous statistical confidence.

For short conversion tracts and micro-recombinations, standard bridge tests can dilute localized signals across multi-kilobase chromosomes.
Cassettes require scale-space filters.
We therefore convolve the differential attention flux with a bank of zero-mean dyadic matched filter wavelets evaluated across scales from 15 to 500 nucleotides, while tracking running drawdowns to pinpoint rapid momentum collapses (see Materials and Methods, Section~\ref{subsec:methods_scalespace_drawdown}).
Candidate breakpoints undergo physical force field deconvolution, measuring dynamic metric velocity reversals along the parental dipole axis (see Materials and Methods, Section~\ref{subsec:methods_force_field}).
Lineage transitions invert momentum.
To prevent over-segmentation in highly diverged cohorts, mode peeling halts when the residual singular value falls below the Marchenko-Pastur bulk spectral edge of random matrix theory \cite{marchenko1967distribution}.
Finally, a profile maximum likelihood polisher refines candidate changepoints down to single-nucleotide coordinates, reporting the exact informational plateau where flanking parental substitutions cease to provide discriminative phylogenetic information (see Materials and Methods, Section~\ref{subsec:methods_polisher}).
