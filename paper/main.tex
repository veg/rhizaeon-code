\abstract{Homologous recombination transfers genetic information between genomes and blurs our perception of the evolutionary relationships between biological taxa. Because recombination undermines phylogenetic inference, diverse approaches have been devised either to partition genome alignments into mostly recombination-free sub-regions, or to strip recombinationally acquired sequence tracts from individual recombinant genomes. The most popular methods suffer from severe computational constraints as dataset sizes increase: $\mathcal{O}(L^B \cdot N^3)$ for $B$ breakpoints across $N$ taxa of alignment length $L$ for whole-alignment partitioning methods like GARD, and $\mathcal{O}(N^3 L)$ for individual recombination-event detection methods like 3SEQ. Here we introduce \RhizAeon, an open-source framework for detecting and characterizing individual recombination events and reticulate evolution through continuous trajectory mechanics across genetic distance metric embeddings in linear time ($\mathcal{O}(N K L)$ for $K$ reference landmarks). Across simulated datasets designed to reflect common failure modes of recombination detection methods, \RhizAeon retains <2\% false-positive rates, overall detection power close to that of 3SEQ (the most powerful method in its class), and better accuracy than 3SEQ in characterizing complex recombinant genomes having >2 breakpoints or more than three parents. Analyses of Sarbecovirus whole genomes, multidrug-resistant \textit{Streptococcus pneumoniae} chromosomes (2.22~Mb), and primate \textit{HLA} Class~I genes accurately capture in seconds published inferences of whole-genome recombination patterns, including complex multi-breakpoint mosaics with three or more parents and sequence transfers ranging from <100 to >70,000 nucleotides. \RhizAeon is accessible as a command-line interface for analysis pipelines or as an interactive WebAssembly graphical application that runs within any modern web browser.}

\keywords{Recombination, Reticulate Evolution, Sequence Manifolds, Continuous Trajectory Tracking, Multidimensional Scaling, Ancestral Recombination Graph}

\otherabstract[Significance Statement]{Homologous recombination and horizontal gene transfer reshuffle genetic material across viruses, bacteria, and eukaryotes, violating the tree-like assumptions of standard phylogenetic analysis. Existing tools for detecting recombination either scale exponentially with breakpoint count or scale cubically with the number of genomes, creating a severe bottleneck for modern population-genomic datasets. We present \RhizAeon, a mathematical framework and open-source software platform that reformulates recombination detection as continuous trajectory tracking across genetic distance spaces in linear time, enabling accurate whole-genome recombination mapping across hundreds of viral, bacterial, and eukaryotic genomes in seconds—both from the command line and interactively inside a web browser.}
