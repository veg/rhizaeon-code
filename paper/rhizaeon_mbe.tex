\abstract{Homologous recombination and horizontal gene transfer assemble mosaic chromosomes from divergent evolutionary lineages, shattering the assumption of bifurcating trees. Existing detection methods remain trapped between computationally prohibitive phylogenetic changepoint algorithms that scale exponentially with breakpoint count and isolated triplet heuristics that trigger high false positive rates under mutational rate variation. We introduce \RhizAeon, an open-source framework that maps multiple sequence alignments into continuous metric spaces, tracking sequence affinities along chromosomes as continuous trajectories without navigating discrete trees or enumerating triplets. By coupling rotary phase embeddings with an unpolarized ancestral root token that absorbs autapomorphic noise, \RhizAeon deconvolves reticulation through physical force field dynamics in linear time. Across a grand simulation sweep of 150,000 whole genomes permuting diverse divergence, tract lengths, and extreme Gamma rate variation, \RhizAeon maintained a false positive rate of 0.415\% on 75,000 non-recombinant nulls, localized physical breakpoints within 16.0 nucleotides, and sustained throughput exceeding 30 genomes per second. Empirical applications across bacterial pathogens, Sarbecovirus spike chimeras, and primate MHC Class I immunogenomics resolve multi-megabase chromosomes and deep multi-domain reticulations in fractions of a second. Compiled to native Rust binaries and client-side WebAssembly, \RhizAeon executes real-time reticulation surveillance within modern web browsers without server data transfer.}
