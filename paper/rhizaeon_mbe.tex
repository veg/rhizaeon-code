\abstract{Homologous recombination and horizontal gene transfer assemble mosaic chromosomes across divergent lineages. Reticulate evolution violates bifurcating trees, yet existing detectors enforce a stark trade-off: phylogenetic changepoint models optimize partitioned topologies at exponential computational cost, whereas sequence triplet heuristics evaluate rapidly but suffer from severe combinatorial event overcounting. We introduce \RhizAeon, an open-source platform that deconvolves reticulate evolution through continuous trajectory mechanics across sequence manifolds in linear time ($\mathcal{O}(N K L)$). By projecting aligned sequences into metric space and routing lineage-specific rate variation into an unpolarized ancestral root sink, the algorithm pinpoints crossover boundaries through centered Brownian-bridge statistics, multi-scale dyadic matched filters, and profile maximum-likelihood refinement. Across canonical coalescent benchmarks and a continuous parameter sweep of 1,000,000 whole genomes, \RhizAeon bounds false-positive rates on non-recombinant nulls below $0.58\%$ ($0.37\%$ across 250,000 heterotachy trials) and consolidates descendant swarms into calibrated ancestral reticulations. Empirical analyses across wildlife sarbecovirus Spike chimeras, multidrug-resistant \textit{Streptococcus pneumoniae} chromosomes (2.22~Mb), and 379 primate \textit{HLA} Class~I alleles reconstruct complex whole-genome mosaicism, cassette exchanges, and micro-conversions in fractions of a second. The core engine compiles to WebAssembly for interactive execution within standard web browsers.}
