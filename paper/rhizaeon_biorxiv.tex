\begin{abstract}
\noindent Homologous recombination, a process that enables the transfer of genetic information between genomes, is pervasive within the biosphere and frequently blurs our perception of the evolutionary relationships between biological taxa. Because recombination undermines the accuracy of phylogenetic inference, a multitude of different approaches have been devised to either partition genome alignment datasets into mostly recombination-free sub-regions, or to strip recombinationally-acquired sequence tracts from individual recombinant genomes. The most popular of these methods all suffer from severe computational constraints as dataset sizes increase: $\mathcal{O}(L^K \cdot N^3)$ for $K$ breakpoints across $N$ taxa of alignment length $L$ for whole-alignment partitioning methods like GARD and $\mathcal{O}(N^3 L)$ for individual recombination-event detection methods like 3SEQ. Here we introduce \RhizAeon, an open-source framework for detecting and characterizing individual recombination events and reticulate evolution through continuous trajectory mechanics across genetic distance metric embeddings in linear time ($\mathcal{O}(N K L)$). Across a range of simulated datasets designed specifically to reflect the common failure modes of recombination detection methods, \RhizAeon retains <2\% false-positive rates, an overall degree of recombination detection power close to that of 3SEQ (the most powerful method in its class), and better accuracy than 3SEQ in characterizing complex recombinant genomes having >2 breakpoints or more than three parents. Analyses of real-world datasets including Sarbecovirus whole genomes, multidrug-resistant \textit{Streptococcus pneumoniae} chromosomes (2.22~Mb), and primate \textit{HLA} Class~I genes accurately capture in seconds published inferences of whole-genome recombination patterns including complex multi-breakpoint recombinant mosaics with three or more parents and recombination events involving transfers of sequence tract lengths ranging from less than 100 nucleotides to over 70 thousand nucleotides. \RhizAeon is accessible as either a command-line interface version for incorporation into analysis pipelines or a version compiled in WebAssembly with a graphical user interface that will interactively run within any modern web browser.

\vspace{6pt}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Recombination, Reticulate Evolution, Sequence Manifolds, Continuous Trajectory Tracking, Multidimensional Scaling, Ancestral Recombination Graph
\end{abstract}
