\begin{abstract}
\noindent Homologous recombination transfers genetic material between genomes and obscures the evolutionary relationships among biological taxa. Because recombination distorts phylogenetic inference, existing methods either partition whole alignments into recombination-free segments or strip recombined tracts from individual mosaic genomes. However, the most widely used tools face severe computational bottlenecks as datasets grow: whole-alignment partitioning methods such as GARD scale exponentially with breakpoint count and cubically with the number of genomes, while triplet-scanning methods such as 3SEQ scale cubically with genome count. Here we introduce \RhizAeon, an open-source framework that detects and characterizes recombination events in linear time by tracking continuous trajectories across genetic distance embeddings. Across simulated benchmarks designed to test common failure modes of recombination detectors, \RhizAeon maintains false-positive rates below 2\%, matches the overall detection power of 3SEQ, and more accurately resolves complex mosaic genomes with more than two breakpoints or three or more parents. In empirical analyses of Sarbecovirus genomes, multidrug-resistant \textit{Streptococcus pneumoniae} chromosomes (2.22~Mb), and primate \textit{HLA} Class~I genes, \RhizAeon recovers published whole-genome recombination patterns in seconds, resolving transfers from $<100$ to $>70{,}000$ nucleotides. \RhizAeon is available both as a command-line tool and as an interactive WebAssembly web application.

\vspace{6pt}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Recombination, Reticulate Evolution, Sequence Manifolds, Continuous Trajectory Tracking, Multidimensional Scaling, Ancestral Recombination Graph
\end{abstract}
