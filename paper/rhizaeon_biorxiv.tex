\begin{abstract}
\noindent Homologous recombination and horizontal gene transfer assemble mosaic chromosomes across divergent lineages. Reticulate evolution violates bifurcating trees, yet existing detectors enforce a stark trade-off: phylogenetic changepoint models optimize partitioned topologies at exponential computational cost, whereas sequence triplet heuristics evaluate rapidly but suffer from severe combinatorial event overcounting. We introduce \RhizAeon, an open-source platform that deconvolves reticulate evolution through continuous trajectory mechanics across metric embeddings in linear time ($\mathcal{O}(N K L)$). Projecting aligned sequences into canonical metric coordinates and routing lineage-specific rate variation into an unpolarized root sink, the engine iteratively scrubs mosaic tracts and verifies breakpoints through Brownian-bridge statistics, dyadic matched filters, and maximum-likelihood polishing. Across canonical coalescent benchmarks and a continuous parameter sweep of 1,000,000 whole genomes, \RhizAeon bounds false-positive rates well below the $5.0\%$ nominal ceiling ($0.42\%$ on 1,200 rate-variation nulls and $0.34\%$ across 332,960 continuous sweep nulls). Leave-one-in verification and bidirectional overlap clustering collapse co-recombinant clades into single ancestral reticulations. Empirical analyses across wildlife sarbecovirus Spike chimeras, multidrug-resistant \textit{Streptococcus pneumoniae} chromosomes (2.22~Mb), and 379 primate \textit{HLA} Class~I alleles reconstruct complex whole-genome mosaicism, cassette exchanges, and micro-conversions in seconds. The core engine compiles to WebAssembly for interactive execution within standard web browsers.

\vspace{6pt}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Recombination, Reticulate Evolution, Sequence Manifolds, Continuous Trajectory Tracking, Multidimensional Scaling, Foundation Models
\end{abstract}
